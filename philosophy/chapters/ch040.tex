\chapter{Appeal and Reversible Judgment}
\label{ch:ch040}

Appeal is not redundant repetition. It changes the level at which error is inspected, often shifting from direct fact-finding to procedure, interpretation, or institutional jurisdiction. A decision system without appeal approximates an \textbf{absorbing state}:
\[
J_t\to J_{t+1}=J_t.
\]
\ToyModel\ Once judgment enters state \(J_t\), the system supplies no authorized transition edge back out of it.
Appeal reintroduces those edges into the state space. It allows some judgments to be revised without making every judgment permanently unstable, and thereby distinguishes present authority from irrevocable epistemic finality.
