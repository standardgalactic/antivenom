\chapter{Reknotting After Error}
\label{ch:ch082}

This chapter treats correction after institutional falsehood as a structural repair problem. Once a system has collapsed onto a mistaken narrative, replacing one proposition is not enough: records, reputations, precedents, and downstream decisions have already been tied to the error. \textbf{Reknotting} names the needed response, modeled as a constrained transformation of the dependency graph that preserves valid subsequent relations while removing dependence upon the false judgment.

The image matters because institutional error propagates through linked authorizations rather than remaining local. Repair must therefore detach what was wrongly downstreamed from the bad judgment without destroying later relations that remain sound. Reknotting is neither simple restoration nor total reset; it is a publicly constrained reweaving of dependencies so that the institution can remember the error, undo its illegitimate inheritances, and continue without preserving the false knot at its core.
