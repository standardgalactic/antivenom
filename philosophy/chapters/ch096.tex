\chapter{The Abuse of Presumption}
\label{ch:ch096}

Presumptions allocate burdens, but they can harden into immunity. This chapter studies when protective defaults must yield to accumulated evidence and how that transition can occur without abandoning procedural restraint. Its focus is on the corruption that arises when presumptions meant to economize inquiry become status shields for officials, institutions, or favored narratives.

The guiding question is how a rebuttable presumption stays genuinely rebuttable. A defensible default may organize action while evidence is sparse; an abusive default keeps reallocating credibility upward even after contradictory material accumulates. The chapter therefore closes the volume by asking when evidence has become strong enough to dislodge a standing presumption without collapsing into the opposite mistake of treating every accusation as self-validating.
