\chapter{Freedom as Protected Indeterminacy}
\label{ch:ch105}

Freedom includes the ability not to have one's social meaning exhaustively fixed by a partial record. A person should not become identical, for institutional purposes, with the first dossier, profile, or episode that happens to be available. Protected indeterminacy names the legal and political space in which incomplete descriptions are prevented from hardening into total identities.

This protection does not erase responsibility. It does not say that action is unknowable or that judgment is impossible. It says instead that responsibility must be assigned without pretending that any partial account captures the whole person. Freedom persists where institutions leave room for revision, surprise, and unfinished self-description.
