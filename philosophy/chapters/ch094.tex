\chapter{Epistemic Nihilism}
\label{ch:ch094}

Causal complexity cannot imply that nothing is knowable. This chapter responds to the temptation to treat manipulation, conflict, and incomplete evidence as reasons to collapse every judgment into arbitrary interpretation. Its task is to establish levels of warranted confidence and to distinguish incomplete knowledge from the claim that disciplined comparison has become impossible.

That distinction matters because nihilism often presents itself as sophistication while quietly equalizing better and worse explanations. The chapter therefore asks how much can be responsibly inferred from convergent but imperfect records, what confidence levels different institutions may claim, and why the admission of uncertainty is not the same thing as surrendering the possibility of reasoned ranking.
