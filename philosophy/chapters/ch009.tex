\chapter{Compression, Salience, and Loss}
\label{ch:ch009}


Records rarely remain in their initial form. They move through successive compressions,
\[
R_0\to R_1\to\cdots\to R_k,
\]
by which raw events become footage, footage becomes selected clips, clips become reports, reports become headlines, and headlines become remembered claims. Each stage reduces bandwidth while increasing apparent intelligibility, making coordination easier at the cost of background detail and alternative lines of inquiry.

To track that cost, the chapter defines a distortion functional
\[
D(R_i,R_{i+1};Q),
\]
where \(Q\) is the class of questions later asked of the record. Compression that is harmless for one question may destroy the evidence needed for another. No summary is universally sufficient, because every gain in salience is also a wager about which future questions will matter.
