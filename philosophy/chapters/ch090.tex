\chapter{Historical Reconstruction}
\label{ch:ch090}

Historical understanding matures over decades rather than hearings or news cycles. This chapter emphasizes the long temporal horizon over which archives open, witnesses speak, technical methods improve, and formerly peripheral facts become legible. The result is not a romance of eventual certainty, but a model of inquiry in which the evidentiary field itself changes as institutions declassify records, archaeological methods advance, and suppressed voices enter the archive.

On that horizon, gaps and silences are neither transparent proof nor excuses for invention. The chapter studies how historians move among newly opened files, late testimony, material remains, and revised interpretive frames, showing why responsible reconstruction must remain revisable for far longer than most legal or political judgments can wait.
