\chapter{Manufactured Uncertainty}
\label{ch:ch063}


\textbf{Manufactured uncertainty} names a tactic by which power exploits skepticism itself. If every causal claim is described as too complex, too entangled, or too contestable ever to justify action, then accountability can be postponed indefinitely without anyone having to supply a better explanation.

The chapter distinguishes legitimate uncertainty from manufactured doubt by a practical criterion: whether the disputing actors clarify what evidence would resolve the issue. Honest uncertainty leaves open a path by which inquiry could discriminate among explanations. Manufactured uncertainty erodes that path, multiplying doubt while refusing to specify any resolving test. The preface claim is therefore that disciplined paranoia must be skeptical of skepticism when ambiguity functions not as intellectual caution but as a strategy for making judgment impossible.
