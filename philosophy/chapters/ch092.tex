\chapter{When Privacy Conceals Domination}
\label{ch:ch092}

Privacy must not become an unreviewable jurisdiction. This chapter studies domestic, corporate, religious, military, and governmental secrecy as settings in which evidence can be prevented from entering public consideration at all. Its target is not privacy as such, but the conversion of opacity into a shield for domination by actors who control testimony, records, and exit.

The problem is structural: the more fully a domain can define its own confidentiality, discipline its own witnesses, and suppress its own documentation, the less likely it is that even serious abuse will become publicly legible. The chapter therefore asks how outside review, protected reporting, and limited disclosure can be introduced without pretending that every private sphere is simply another arm of the state.
