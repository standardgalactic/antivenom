\chapter{Refusal as a Positive Operation}
\label{ch:ch043}

Refusal is not the absence of decision. It is a positive legal operation that prevents an inadequately supported transition:
\[
\mathsf R:(E,A)\mapsto \neg(E\vdash A).
\]
\Definition\ The \textbf{refusal operator} \(\mathsf R\) gives law a way to say ``not yet,'' ``not by this route,'' or ``not on this evidence.''
In the Spherepop distinction, that matters because active refusal is different from mere failure to continue: it preserves the burden on institutions to justify intrusion without conscripting the subject into producing the case. A legal system that cannot formalize refusal has only crude acceptance and rejection, and loses the intermediate protections on which procedural liberty depends.
