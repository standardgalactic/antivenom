\chapter{Merge, Link, and Unlink}
\label{ch:ch049}

This chapter develops the derived operations of \textbf{merge}, \textbf{link}, and \textbf{unlink} for evidentiary synthesis. To merge two files is stronger than to link them: merge fuses separate items into one operative object, while link preserves distinct records under a relation that can still be inspected. Unlinking a record from a claim does not erase the record, because the historical trace of association can remain even after the association is withdrawn.

That distinction supports a non-destructive model of correction. A false association can be revoked without pretending the historical association never occurred. The chapter therefore treats relational repair as a core institutional task: many large errors come not from missing facts, but from having linked, merged, or failed to unlink appearances in the wrong way.
