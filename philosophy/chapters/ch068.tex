\chapter{The Permanent Record and the Mutable Person}
\label{ch:ch068}


A person changes while the record persists. The outline models this disparity as the difference between human change \(X(t)\) and a frozen representation \(R(t_0)\) retrieved again and again after its original moment. Digital systems can keep returning \(R(t_0)\) to the present, thereby collapsing biographical time and encouraging institutions to treat a past snapshot as a standing portrait.
\ToyModel\ The trajectory/archive pair models temporal misfit between persons and durable records.

The central question is when an accurate record becomes contextually false in continued use. \(R(t_0)\) may remain true of what occurred at \(t_0\), yet misleading when used as if it fully represented the later person described by \(X(t)\). The chapter's preface thus frames the permanent record not as a simple contest between truth and forgetting, but as a problem of temporal fit between durable archives and mutable persons.
