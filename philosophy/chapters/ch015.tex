\chapter{Computational Irreducibility and Legal Fact}
\label{ch:ch015}

Some legally relevant facts are governed by \textbf{computational irreducibility}: their consequences cannot be inferred substantially faster than the underlying process can unfold. In such domains, long-term perspective cannot be replaced by a sufficiently clever snapshot, because the legally important structure appears only through staged interaction rather than through an ex ante summary.

The chapter separates strong claims of undecidability from the more practical limits of intractability. It focuses on NP-hard search, model uncertainty, chaotic sensitivity, strategic opacity, and the impossibility of specifying a complete state description in advance. The lesson is not mystification about complexity, but procedural humility: when social processes can only be learned by running them, law must rely on revisable findings, staged observation, and institutions that can absorb information that honest inquiry could not have front-loaded.
