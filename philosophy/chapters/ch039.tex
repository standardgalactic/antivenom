\chapter{The Judge, the Jury, and Distributed Error}
\label{ch:ch039}

Judges and juries embody different error structures. A single expert decision-maker may produce consistency, but it also creates a concentrated failure point in which one bias or blind spot can propagate through the whole inferential chain. A group may diversify judgment while introducing conformity effects, polarization, and other collective pathologies. The chapter therefore studies \textbf{Condorcet-style aggregation} while refusing the simplifying fiction that juror errors are independent and identically distributed. The design problem is not to choose between pure expertise and pure democracy, but to distribute error so that no single recurrent failure mode becomes sovereign.
