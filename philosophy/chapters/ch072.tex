\chapter{Premature-Collapse Loss}
\label{ch:ch072}

This chapter defines the cost of committing too early to a single institutional story. Let \(\tau\) be the time of commitment, let \(\widehat H_\tau\) be the history endorsed at that time, and let \(H^\ast\) be the eventual best-supported history. The \textbf{premature-collapse loss} is
\[
L_{\mathrm{collapse}}(\tau)
=
C\!\left(\widehat H_\tau,H^\ast\right)
+
\alpha A(\tau)
+
\beta R(\tau),
\]
\ToyModel\ where \(C\) measures inferential error, \(A\) the harm of actions authorized by that error, and \(R\) the cost of reversing an entrenched judgment once institutions have reorganized themselves around it.

The point is not that every delay is good. It is that haste creates a compound loss: a mistaken inference, the downstream actions taken under false confidence, and the additional burden of undoing a judgment once it has sedimented into records, procedures, or force. By writing that structure explicitly as \(L_{\mathrm{collapse}}(\tau)\), the chapter prepares the next step: determining how far any body of evidence may legitimately travel before it licenses this kind of costly closure.
