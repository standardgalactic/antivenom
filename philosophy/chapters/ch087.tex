\chapter{Child Protection and Family Privacy}
\label{ch:ch087}

Child protection makes the privacy--publicity conflict especially severe. Privacy can shield intimacy and family autonomy, but it can also conceal abuse; publicity can surface danger, but indiscriminate exposure can produce irreversible harm. This chapter studies asymmetric evidence in family settings through mandatory reporting, confidential testimony, clinical observation, school-based warning signs, and bounded disclosure.

Its focus is on how weak, unevenly distributed signals should be combined without converting every suspicion into a license for invasive intervention. The chapter therefore asks how reporting duties, confidential channels, case review, and disclosure limits can be designed so that children are not trapped inside opaque authority while families are not destroyed by the uncontrolled spread of untested accusation.
