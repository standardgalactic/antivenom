\chapter{When Publicity Is Necessary}
\label{ch:ch093}

Some facts must be disclosed before adjudication is complete because delay itself creates continuing harm. This chapter derives the conditions under which provisional publication is justified without confusing publication with final judgment. It is concerned with cases in which private channels predictably bury evidence, isolate victims, or allow ongoing danger to persist while institutions wait for a completeness they have no intention of creating.

The issue, then, is not whether publicity is risky; it plainly is. The question is when investigative reporting, public warning, registries, or open documentation supply epistemic conditions that closed systems refuse to supply for themselves. The chapter argues that such publication can be warranted as a provisional, reviewable act when the alternative is not fairness but the durable invisibility of harm.
