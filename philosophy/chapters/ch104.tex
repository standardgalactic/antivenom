\chapter{Institutions That Can Say “Not Yet”}
\label{ch:ch104}

A mature institution must be able to say ``not yet.'' It must distinguish delay from refusal, refusal from denial, and provisional action from final judgment. Without those distinctions, every pause looks like evasion and every temporary measure risks masquerading as settled conclusion.

The chapter therefore proposes explicit procedural states for incomplete evidence together with deadlines for reconsideration. Such states let an institution acknowledge uncertainty without losing track of its duties: it can defer, preserve the question, protect against interim harm, and return on schedule. The competence to say ``not yet'' is neither paralysis nor indifference, but a disciplined way of refusing premature closure.
