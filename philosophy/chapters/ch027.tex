\chapter{Algorithmic Salience}
\label{ch:ch027}

Ranking systems implement a \textbf{salience function}
\[
\sigma(x\mid u,t,\theta),
\]
\Definition\ conditioned on user \(u\), time \(t\), and model parameters \(\theta\). What rises through the hierarchy is therefore not what is intrinsically important, but what is important relative to an objective function. Recurrence can be an artifact of optimization rather than evidentiary force.

The chapter distinguishes engagement salience, investigative salience, legal salience, scientific salience, and historical salience. Their divergence explains why publicly dominant evidence need not be probatively strong. Any politics of publicity must therefore scrutinize the objective functions and feedback loops that allocate notice before explicit judgment begins.
