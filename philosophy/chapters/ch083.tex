\chapter{Criminal Investigation}
\label{ch:ch083}

Criminal investigation is the clearest ordinary setting in which suspicion is both necessary and morally dangerous. This chapter applies the framework to eyewitness testimony, forensic evidence, confessions, surveillance footage, prosecutorial disclosure, and wrongful conviction. Its emphasis is structural rather than sensational: early hypotheses do not merely interpret evidence after the fact; they help determine which witnesses are re-interviewed, which traces are tested, which videos are reviewed, and which inconsistencies are ignored.

The chapter therefore treats investigation as a sequence of constrained updates rather than a march from clue to certainty. It asks how lineup practice, forensic testimony, confession procedure, video interpretation, and disclosure obligations should be organized when the gravest miscarriages often arise from institutions that mistake a guided search for a neutral discovery process.
