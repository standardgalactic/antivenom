\chapter{Unknown Unknowns}
\label{ch:ch016}

Ordinary probability models distribute likelihood over a given hypothesis space, but inquiry also faces \textbf{unknown unknowns}: cases in which the relevant explanation is absent from the model itself,
\[
H^\ast\notin\mathcal H.
\]
\Definition\ This condition defines model incompleteness rather than ordinary uncertainty over listed options.
This chapter distinguishes risk, uncertainty, ambiguity, ignorance, and model incompleteness. Its claim is that many investigative failures arise not from misweighting listed options, but from treating the current menu of options as complete when it is not.

That is why legal procedure cannot be satisfied with a single closed pass through a case. If \(\mathcal H\) may omit the truth, then reopening, appeal, anomaly reporting, and adversarial challenge are not luxuries but safeguards against model closure. The right lesson is disciplined openness to ontology revision, not limitless suspicion: the task is to preserve mechanisms by which an unrepresented hypothesis can become thinkable and then testable.
