\chapter{Delay, Liberty, and the Protected Interval}
\label{ch:ch046}

The period before judgment is not empty time but a \textbf{protected interval} in which competing histories remain institutionally possible. Let \(\mathcal H_t\) be the \emph{admissible history set} at time \(t\). As evidence is tested, that set may shrink:
\[
\mathcal H_{t+1}\subseteq\mathcal H_t.
\]
\Definition\ Legitimate judgment requires that the shrinkage occur through warranted exclusions---through admissibility, testing, burden, and authorized inference---rather than through the mere disappearance of unpopular alternatives under time pressure.
Delay protects liberty when it preserves the interval in which those histories can still be publicly defended before action becomes irreversible.
