## Claim
Separation of powers is a regime of structured non-identical observation that prevents correlated collapse by distributing information, incentives, temporal horizons, and error modes.


## Depends on
- **ch047, "Record, Verify, Recognize, Authorize"** — trustworthy state change requires distinct operations rather than one office converting appearance directly into consequence.
- **ch048, "Pop, Refuse, Bind, Collapse"** — credible institutions need lawful ways to deny, commit, unwind, and detect collapse.
- **ch049, "Merge, Link, and Unlink"** — institutional intelligence depends on composing relations without erasing provenance or reversibility.
- **ch050, "The Admissibility Boundary"** — each forum must decide what may enter its reasoning before it can act responsibly.
- **ch051, "Continuation Geometry"** — continuity depends on authorized pathways, not mere duration or assertion.
- **ch052, "Distinction Holonomy"** — even preserved continuities can accumulate path-dependent drift that must be checked by crossing institutional boundaries.

## Load-bearing steps
1. The chapter recasts separation of powers as structured non-identical observation: different institutions see the same public matter through different information sets, incentives, time horizons, and error modes.
2. Because those perspectives are nonidentical, public judgment is not forced through a single inferential pipeline from allegation to consequence.
3. The aim is not perfect institutional independence but the prevention of correlated collapse, in which all offices inherit the same blind spot or premature closure at once.
4. Institutional redundancy succeeds only when disagreement can persist long enough to expose hidden assumptions that a unified authority would normalize.
5. Separation of powers therefore functions as an epistemic ecology whose chief product is inspectable disagreement rather than seamless administrative unity.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** Separation of powers is mainly a moral-political check on tyranny, so recasting it as epistemic design understates its normative purpose. **Response:** The chapter's point is that the anti-tyranny effect operates partly through epistemic nonidentity: divided procedures prevent one line of authority from self-validating its own suspicions.
