# Spine: ch031 — Contextual Integrity

**Volume:** III: Attention and Publicity
**Part:** VI. Privacy as Boundary and Capacity
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Contextual integrity evaluates information flow as \(\tau=(s,r,\alpha,c,p)\), so privacy violation is often an unauthorized change in sender, recipient, type, context, or transmission principle rather than revelation alone.

## Depends on
- ch029 — privacy is a matter of preserving boundaries that sustain agency and social roles.
- ch030 — privacy loss often arises from aggregation and linkage rather than any single exposed secret.

## Load-bearing steps
1. The chapter represents each information flow by the tuple \(\tau=(s,r,\alpha,c,p)\), identifying sender, recipient, information type, context, and transmission principle.
2. Legitimacy depends on the integrity of that whole tuple, not simply on whether the content \(\alpha\) was once visible somewhere.
3. The same fact can be permissible in one \(\tau\) and violative in another because shifts in \(r\), \(c\), or \(p\) alter the role-structure and purpose of the transfer.
4. Many privacy injuries are therefore unauthorized changes in \(\tau\) rather than first revelations of secrets.
5. The framework replaces the blunt public/private binary with a structured test of who may know what, in which role, and under which transmission rule.

## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Medical information passed from a clinician to an employer or advertiser | Illustrative | Shows unchanged content becoming wrongful when roles and purposes shift. |
| Student records repurposed outside the educational setting | Illustrative | Makes context-sensitive transmission norms concrete. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- Objection: contextual norms often encode local prejudice or inertia, so grounding privacy in them simply ratifies existing power relations.
- Response: context supplies the grammar of evaluation, not its final moral verdict; transmission norms remain open to criticism when the roles or purposes themselves are unjust.
