# Spine: ch083 — Criminal Investigation

## Claim
Criminal investigation is justified only when it treats eyewitnesses, forensic traces, confessions, surveillance footage, and disclosure duties as staged inputs to inquiry rather than as early hypotheses ratifying themselves on the road to wrongful conviction.

## Depends on
- ch082 (Reknotting After Error) — repaired institutions should remember failure by redesigning procedure rather than returning to naive trust in first impressions.
- ch035 (The Architecture of Proof) — coercive judgment needs a public structure from suspicion to action, not a fact-finder's inner certainty.
- ch036 (Burdens and Standards of Proof) — different stages of action assign different burdens for moving uncertainty before force is used.
- ch042 (Warrants and the Cost of Looking) — search powers must formulate reasons before discovery rather than laundering reasons out of what a search turns up.
- ch044 (Due Process as Constrained Updating) — vivid early evidence may justify only limited revision until its evidentiary reach has been tested.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Criminal cases are assembled from eyewitness testimony, physical traces, confessions, surveillance footage, and disclosure-sensitive files, all of which can look probative while still underdetermining cause or perpetrator.
2. Early investigative hypotheses shape which lineups are run, which forensic tests are ordered, which videos are reviewed, and which exculpatory leads are neglected, so collection itself is theory-laden.
3. Because the same item of evidence may justify a lead while falling far short of conviction, institutions must distinguish inquiry, search, arrest, charge, and verdict instead of letting one threshold swallow the rest.
4. Eyewitness error, forensic overstatement, confession contamination, and suppressed disclosure are recurring mechanisms of wrongful conviction because they let a guided story masquerade as neutral accumulation.
5. Criminal procedure earns legitimacy only when it keeps those mechanisms publicly contestable and revisable before suspicion is allowed to harden into coercive certainty.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** Violent crime is too time-sensitive for all this staged caution; if officials cannot act on strong hunches, dangerous people stay free. **Response:** The argument allows fast, low-level action on limited evidence; it denies only the collapse of inquiry, intrusion, accusation, and punishment into one undifferentiated threshold.
