# Spine: ch034 — Privacy and Future Reinterpretation

**Volume:** III: Attention and Publicity
**Part:** VI. Privacy as Boundary and Capacity
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Because record meaning shifts over time according to \(M_t(R) \neq M_{t+\Delta}(R)\), privacy must protect against future reinterpretation through forgetting, expiration, and barriers to recombination.

## Depends on
- ch029 — privacy protects bounded spaces of agency rather than mere concealment.
- ch030 — recombination and accumulation can generate invasive new meanings.
- ch031 — legitimacy of informational use depends on context-sensitive limits, not bare exposure.
- ch032 — durable models built from traces intensify the power of cross-temporal reuse.
- ch033 — persons need underdescription and revisability rather than permanent exhaustive legibility.

## Load-bearing steps
1. The chapter defines temporal reinterpretation through \(M_t(R)\), the meaning assigned to record \(R\) at time \(t\), and insists that \(M_t(R) \neq M_{t+\Delta}(R)\) can hold even when \(R\) itself is unchanged.
2. Because meaning shifts with norms, institutions, and analytic tools, preserved traces can acquire fresh accusatory or exclusionary force without any new underlying act.
3. Persistent archives and identifiers make it possible to recombine old records into new mosaics and models, linking this chapter back to the interaction terms of ch030 and the action pipeline of ch032.
4. Privacy therefore protects against unknown future classificatory regimes, not just against present disclosure under current social ontology.
5. The institutional consequence is a case for forgetting, expiration, expungement, and friction against perpetual recombination rather than universal permanent recall.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- Objection: durable archives are essential for accountability, history, and learning, so built-in forgetting mainly benefits those who wish to escape deserved judgment.
- Response: some targeted retention is justified, but default indefinite preservation of ordinary personal traces is disproportionate; accountability needs can be met more narrowly than permanent universal recall.
