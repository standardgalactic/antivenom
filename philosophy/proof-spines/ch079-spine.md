# Spine: ch079 — Curvature of Reinterpretation

**Volume:** VII: Formal Synthesis
**Part:** XIV. Dynamics, Geometry, and Holonomy
**Status:** spine

## Claim
The curvature of reinterpretation is the commutator $[\nabla_i,\nabla_j]J=\nabla_i\nabla_jJ-\nabla_j\nabla_iJ$, which measures order-sensitive judgment directly.

## Depends on
- ch077 (Belief-State Manifolds) — supplies the geometric space in which reinterpretation can deform neighborhoods of belief
- ch078 (Nonintegrability and Institutional Path Dependence) — shows that routes matter, making local bending rather than path-independent translation the right model of revision

## Load-bearing steps
1. Represent distinct evidentiary or interpretive moves by update operators $\nabla_i$ and $\nabla_j$ acting on judgment $J$.
2. Define curvature through the commutator $[\nabla_i,\nabla_j]J=\nabla_i\nabla_jJ-\nabla_j\nabla_iJ$.
3. Interpret a nonzero commutator as formal proof that opposite orders of otherwise admissible updates yield different judgments.
4. Use that noncommutativity to model the legal importance of sequence, framing, and procedural posture as geometric rather than merely rhetorical facts.
5. Conclude that reinterpretation bends judgment locally, preparing the transition from curved updates to RSVP fields of public interpretation.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** "Curvature" sounds like decorative mathematics unless the chapter can specify a real metric structure; otherwise the argument risks becoming metaphor piled on metaphor.
- **Response:** The chapter needs only the structural claim that reinterpretation deforms neighborhoods unevenly; a full tensorial formalism can remain deferred without losing that point.
