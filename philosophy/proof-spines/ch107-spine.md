# Spine: ch107 — Equality of Evidentiary Standing

**Volume:** X: Normative Conclusions
**Part:** XVIII. Freedom Under Causal Opacity
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Equality requires evidentiary standing: practical access to collecting, preserving, interpreting, and presenting the materials from which institutional reality is made.

## Depends on
- ch106 (The Right to Contest the Model) — contestation is empty if some speakers lack the credibility or procedural access needed to make their challenge matter.
- ch036 (Burdens and Standards of Proof) — institutions allocate who must move uncertainty and by how much before force may be used.
- ch091 (When Procedure Protects Power) — formally identical procedures are not fair when wealth, rank, dependence, or fear stratify who can use them.
- ch093 (When Publicity Is Necessary) — public exposure can be justified when private channels systematically bury dispersed testimony and pattern evidence.
- ch087 (Child Protection and Family Privacy) — institutions routinely remember visible wrongful intervention more vividly than hidden nonintervention, distorting whose evidence is treated as urgent.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Because formal equality leaves power asymmetries untouched, the chapter defines evidentiary standing as practical access to collecting, preserving, interpreting, and presenting evidence.
2. Because institutional reality is produced by those operations, unequal ability to make a record, keep it alive, and force it into decision means unequal standing even when hearing rights are formally equal.
3. Because credibility, storage, expertise, and procedural access are unevenly distributed, some actors can manufacture institutional fact far more easily than others.
4. Therefore equality requires more than permission to speak: it requires materially comparable access to the evidentiary machinery itself.

## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Public-defender versus privately financed defense capacity | Illustrative | Highlights how nominally equal adjudicative rights diverge when one side can actually develop and present evidence. |
| Workplace or university complaint systems requiring extensive self-documentation from precarious complainants | Illustrative | Shows credibility and record-building burdens distributed unequally before any formal hearing begins. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** Equality of evidentiary standing sounds like a demand to equalize credibility outcomes, forcing institutions to believe claims irrespective of their quality.
- **Response:** The chapter defends equality in the ability to place challenge and rebuttal into collective judgment, not insulation from evaluation once evidence is there.
