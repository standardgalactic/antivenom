# Spine: ch100 — Attention-Aware Archives

**Volume:** IX: Institutional Design
**Part:** XVII. Designing for Delayed Truth
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Archives should make provenance, uncertainty, duplication, dissent, and discovery history recoverable so later investigators can find what earlier attention suppressed.

## Depends on
- ch099 (Bounded Transparency) — making reasons visible is insufficient unless the visible record is usable under real cognitive limits.
- ch025 (Attention as a Scarce Public Resource) — whoever structures attention partly structures what a society can know.
- ch056 (Archives and Institutional Memory) — archives are active arrangements of preservation, indexing, loss, and access rather than passive storehouses.
- ch084 (Public Disorder and Mass Recording) — evidentiary abundance can widen the record while making interpretation harder, not easier.
- ch095 (The Abuse of “More Context”) — demands for ever more material can function as effective deferral when no one can process it in time.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Because later investigators inherit the biases of earlier attention, archives should expose provenance graphs, uncertainty annotations, duplicate clusters, dissenting classifications, and discovery histories.
2. Because a document can be nominally public yet effectively lost, search must be able to recover items that prior salience models suppressed or misranked.
3. Because duplication, dissent, and provenance affect what weight evidence deserves, those structures must be visible rather than flattened into a single file listing.
4. Therefore an attention-aware archive treats findability and contextual recoverability as part of institutional accountability.

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ICIJ/Panama Papers searchable document interfaces | Illustrative | Shows how indexing and cross-linking convert an otherwise unusable document dump into actionable public knowledge. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** Once archivists prioritize and summarize, they become hidden editors of public memory, replacing overload with curation bias.
- **Response:** Attention-aware design must preserve provenance and permit drill-down to source materials, so triage aids navigation rather than silently substituting for the record.
