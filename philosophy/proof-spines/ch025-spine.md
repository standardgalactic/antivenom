# Spine: ch025 — Attention as a Scarce Public Resource

**Volume:** III: Attention and Publicity
**Part:** V. Publicity Without Comprehension
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Publicity is constrained by the attention allocation vector \(\mathbf a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\) with \(\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i \leq A\), so power can operate by capturing shares of \(A\) rather than suppressing speech.

## Depends on
- ch023 — visibility needs mediation before it becomes knowledge.
- ch024 — evidentiary abundance creates search and sorting bottlenecks even before questions of persuasion arise.

## Load-bearing steps
1. The chapter formalizes public attention as the finite allocation vector \(\mathbf a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\) constrained by \(\sum_i a_i \leq A\).
2. Because the budget \(A\) is bounded, additional uptake granted to one issue necessarily withholds uptake from others.
3. Agenda-setting, repetition, and timing are mechanisms for redistributing coordinates of \(\mathbf a\), not merely rhetorical embellishments after evidence is already understood.
4. Powerful actors can dominate inquiry by purchasing, automating, or otherwise concentrating attention shares, or by diluting rivals within the same budget \(A\).
5. Institutions therefore need triage, watchdog specialization, and anti-monopolization norms to keep the public attention vector from being captured.

## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Friday-evening release of adverse reports or filings | Illustrative | Makes timing visible as an epistemic rather than merely public-relations tactic. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- Objection: attention belongs to audiences, not institutions, and attempts to manage its distribution paternalistically interfere with free public choice.
- Response: the chapter treats attention allocation as already engineered by unequal capacities; institutional design is needed to prevent capture of the conditions under which free choice can become informed judgment.
