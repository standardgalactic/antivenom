# Spine: ch009 — Compression, Salience, and Loss

**Volume:** I: The Epistemology of Suspicion
**Part:** II. Events, Records, and Reconstructions
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Compression reorganizes evidence through the sequence \(R_0\to\cdots\to R_k\), and its epistemic cost is captured by the question-relative distortion functional \(D(R_i,R_{i+1};Q)\).

## Depends on
- ch006 — records are already mediated traces rather than transparent stand-ins for events.
- ch007 — each transformation stage can preserve some features while discarding others.
- ch008 — recording itself is selective, so later summarization compounds prior exclusion rather than beginning it.

## Load-bearing steps
1. The chapter models summarization as a compression chain \(R_0\to R_1\to\cdots\to R_k\), in which each step trades bandwidth for apparent intelligibility.
2. It ties concrete stages of that chain---footage to clips, clips to reports, reports to headlines, headlines to remembered claims---to escalating salience and shrinking context.
3. The distortion functional \(D(R_i,R_{i+1};Q)\) supplies the formal hinge, because loss must be measured relative to the later question class \(Q\) rather than in the abstract.
4. From that setup follows the chapter's main conclusion: no compression is universally harmless, since what is negligible for one question may be decisive for another.

## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Executive dashboards built from underlying case files | Illustrative | Shows how salience for decision-makers is gained by stripping away local context and ambiguity. |
| Incident-report summaries replacing full witness statements | Illustrative | Makes visible how alternatives and tone vanish when records are compressed for circulation. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- Large-scale institutions cannot function without compression, so emphasizing loss risks a romantic demand for impossible total context rather than a usable epistemology.
- Response: the argument does not reject compression; it insists that compression's necessity does not erase its costs and that good inquiry must price those costs explicitly.
