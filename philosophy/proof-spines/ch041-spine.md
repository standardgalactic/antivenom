## Claim
The inconvenience principle is the claim that a friction parameter $\phi$ can enlarge liberty when social loss $L(\phi)=L_{\mathrm{false\ action}}(\phi)+L_{\mathrm{missed\ action}}(\phi)+L_{\mathrm{delay}}(\phi)$ is minimized by delaying coercion at points where uncertainty meets irreversible power.

## Depends on
- ch035–ch040 (Part VII, Evidence Before Action) — the chapter assumes coercive action is justified only through a public proof architecture with burdens, admissibility filters, adversarial testing, distributed roles, and authorized revision.
- ch040 (Appeal and Reversible Judgment) — it specifically takes the claim that ex post correction matters, then argues that reversibility alone is insufficient where the initial intrusion or sanction can itself do irreversible damage.

## Load-bearing steps
1. The inconvenience principle starts from the outline's triad of conditions---incomplete information, fallible actors, and asymmetrically costly coercive action---under which friction can enlarge rather than diminish freedom.
2. The chapter introduces a friction parameter $\phi$ to represent the delay or procedural cost imposed before intervention, making ``inconvenience a design variable rather than a slogan.
3. Social loss is then written as $L(\phi)=L_{\mathrm{false\ action}}(\phi)+L_{\mathrm{missed\ action}}(\phi)+L_{\mathrm{delay}}(\phi)$, so false positives, false negatives, and the cost of waiting appear in a single ledger.
4. The shape of $L(\phi)$ explains why neither zero friction nor maximal friction is defensible: too little $\phi$ invites impulsive coercion, while too much $\phi$ blocks legitimate protection.
5. Legal design therefore aims to place friction precisely where epistemic uncertainty and irreversible power intersect, not to glorify delay for its own sake.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** Procedural inconvenience predictably lets dangerous people escape timely intervention and therefore sacrifices public safety to formalism.
- **Response:** That counts only the visible cases where friction blocked immediate action and ignores the systematic false positives and irreversible harms produced by easy power; the chapter argues those costs belong in the same ledger.
