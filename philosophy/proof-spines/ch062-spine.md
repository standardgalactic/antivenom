# Spine: ch062 — Informants, Whistleblowers, and Anonymous Speech

**Volume:** VI: Adversaries, Platforms, and Automated Judgment
**Part:** XI. Untrustworthy Actors
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Anonymity redistributes verification burdens: it can protect corrective information, but anonymous speech earns credibility only through corroboration rather than identity alone.

## Depends on
- ch059 — adversarial settings include retaliation and concealment, so who can safely speak is part of modeling the threat environment.
- ch060 — correlated capture can close official channels across multiple institutions, making outside or anonymous disclosure necessary.
- ch061 — transparency must be designed for audit rather than theater, which makes protected anonymous disclosure one legitimate route by which hidden facts become reviewable.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Start from the dual effect of anonymity: it protects corrective information against retaliation while also removing an ordinary source of accountability.
2. Recast that dual effect as a redistribution of verification burdens from identity toward corroboration, documentary quality, and independent checking.
3. Reject both automatic trust and automatic suspicion by insisting that anonymous speech is neither self-authenticating nor self-discrediting.
4. Conclude that anonymous disclosure is admissible when institutions can shift evaluation onto verifiable content rather than requiring public identity as the sole warrant of credibility.

## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Pentagon Papers sourcing chain | Illustrative | shows why attribution risk can be inseparable from public-interest disclosure |
| Panama Papers / ICIJ anonymous leak | Illustrative | demonstrates anonymous initiation followed by large-scale corroboration |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- Anonymous accusation is too easily weaponized for defamation and manipulation, so public reason should demand attributable speakers before granting civic credibility.
- Response: the chapter does not ask institutions to trust anonymity; it asks them to treat anonymity as admissible only when the underlying claims can be independently checked.
