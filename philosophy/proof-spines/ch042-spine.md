# Spine: ch042 — Warrants and the Cost of Looking

**Volume:** IV: Law as an Epistemic Machine
**Part:** VIII. Legal Friction as Freedom
**Status:** spine

## Claim
A warrant is the bounded search authorization $W=(T,S,Q,\Delta t)$, which makes state looking costly by fixing target, scope, justification, and duration before discovery can retroactively validate the intrusion.

## Depends on
- ch041 (The Inconvenience Principle) — the chapter specializes Part VIII's general claim by showing how one form of procedural friction prices the act of looking itself.

## Load-bearing steps
1. The warrant chapter formalizes ex ante authorization as the tuple $W=(T,S,Q,\Delta t)$, turning a vague desire to search into a bounded object that can be publicly inspected.
2. $T$ and $S$ do different work: the target $T$ identifies who or what may be searched, while scope $S$ constrains how much rummaging the authorization permits once the search begins.
3. The justification term $Q$ encodes the evidentiary showing that must be articulated before the search, preventing discovery from manufacturing its own prior reasons after the fact.
4. The temporal bound $\Delta t$ matters because a warrant without duration is a rolling license to keep looking until something useful appears.
5. By forcing each of these coordinates to be specified in advance, the warrant makes looking costly and contestable, rather than allowing generalized suspicion to expand opportunistically after intrusion starts.

## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| *Entick v. Carrington* (1765) and the anti-general-warrant tradition | Illustrative | Classic anchor for the idea that authorized looking must be particularized in advance rather than left to executive rummaging. |
| Cell-phone search warrant controversies after *Riley v. California* (2014) | Illustrative | Useful modern example of why the cost of looking must scale with the density and recombinability of what can be seen. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** Warrant requirements are formal delays that impede effective policing; competent officers should be trusted to search first when they have a good instinct and justify later.
- **Response:** Outside genuine exigency, that flexibility is exactly how curiosity and administrative appetite become self-ratifying evidence, which is the failure mode the warrant requirement exists to block.
