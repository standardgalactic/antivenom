# Spine: ch020 — Discovery Curves

**Volume:** II: Causal Explosion
**Part:** IV. Time, Delay, and the Maturation of Evidence
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Discovery curves model recognized evidence as \(F(t)\), with domain-specific shapes and occasional jumps \(F(t^\ast+)-F(t^\ast-)\gg 0\), so institutions should judge claims by expected maturation rather than early tempo alone.

## Depends on
- ch017 — evidence matures over time rather than presenting its full force at once.
- ch018 — long durations can carry evidentiary weight that single episodes cannot match.
- ch019 — salience hierarchies distort how institutions interpret the tempo of accumulating evidence.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Because \(F(t)\) measures the fraction of ultimately relevant evidence recognized by time \(t\), discovery has a shape that can be studied instead of being treated as a single moment of revelation.
2. Because exponential, logistic, power-law, and punctuated forms imply different expectations about early gains, plateaus, and late breakthroughs, tempo has no fixed meaning across domains.
3. Because a jump such as \(F(t^\ast+)-F(t^\ast-)\gg 0\) can follow a major archival or forensic discovery, long periods of apparent stasis need not show that inquiry is exhausted.
4. Because institutions misread curve shape when they equate slow accumulation with weakness or fast uptake with truth, standards for persistence, resourcing, and reopening should be keyed to the expected \(F(t)\) of the domain.
