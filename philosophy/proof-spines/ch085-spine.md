# Spine: ch085 — War, Intelligence, and Atrocity Evidence

**Volume:** VIII: Applications and Case Structures
**Part:** XV. Domains of Difficult Judgment
**Status:** spine

## Claim
War and atrocity judgment requires tiered use of damaged archives, propaganda, classified sources, satellite imagery, and delayed forensic access so that morally urgent early reports can guide protection without being mistaken for already-complete public proof.

## Depends on
- ch084 (Public Disorder and Mass Recording) — proliferating recordings widen evidence while leaving role, sequence, and interpretation contested.
- ch073 (Evidentiary Reach) — battlefield and OSINT evidence often travels differently across operational, diplomatic, archival, and prosecutorial decisions.
- ch074 (Update Budgets) — high-salience but weakly authenticated wartime evidence should trigger only proportionate institutional revision.
- ch040 (Appeal and Reversible Judgment) — institutions need ways to act provisionally now without pretending the first attribution is epistemically final.
- ch080 (RSVP Fields of Public Interpretation) — propaganda ecosystems and reputational pressures steer which atrocity narratives harden fastest.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Wartime evidence is structurally damaged and strategic: archives are destroyed, propaganda saturates interpretation, key sources remain classified, and forensic access often arrives late.
2. Satellite imagery, intercepted communications, witness testimony, and battlefield remnants can each be morally urgent without yet being complete enough for the same downstream use.
3. Chain-of-command inference is especially difficult because responsibility often must be reconstructed from patterns of orders, logistics, and correlated attacks rather than from a single dispositive document.
4. The theory must therefore explain how early reports can justify warning, humanitarian response, or provisional attribution while still remaining open to revision as delayed forensic and archival evidence emerges.
5. Disciplined paranoia in war means neither waiting for perfect knowledge nor laundering strategic fragments into courtroom-grade certainty before their reach has been tested.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** If atrocity naming waits for too much proof, perpetrators exploit the delay and victims are abandoned. **Response:** The chapter lowers thresholds for warning and protection, but raises them again for punishment and archival finality.
