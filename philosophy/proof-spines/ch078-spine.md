# Spine: ch078 — Nonintegrability and Institutional Path Dependence

**Volume:** VII: Formal Synthesis
**Part:** XIV. Dynamics, Geometry, and Holonomy
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Institutional path dependence is the nonintegrability of the admissible distribution $\mathcal D$, so the same informational materials can yield different endpoints when traversed in different procedural orders.

## Depends on
- ch077 (Belief-State Manifolds) — provides the geometric picture in which inquiry is a path through a structured space rather than a single before-and-after jump
- ch074 (Update Budgets) — carries forward the idea that the permissibility of later movement depends on how earlier evidence has already spent the revision allowance

## Load-bearing steps
1. Start from the admissible distribution $\mathcal D\subseteq T\mathcal M$ and ask whether institutional motion integrates to path-independent surfaces or remains sequence-sensitive.
2. Show that when hearing an accusation before exculpatory review yields a different endpoint than the reverse order, the same dossier has generated different outcomes because procedural sequence changed the meaning of later evidence.
3. Characterize this order-sensitivity as nonintegrability: there is no route-independent summary of movement through the constrained belief-state manifold.
4. Connect the resulting residue to \emph{narrative hysteresis} and \textbf{distinction holonomy}, where traveling around a procedural loop returns an institution to an apparently familiar issue with altered classificatory orientation.
5. Conclude that institutional path dependence is structural, not accidental, and must be modeled before curvature can be computed.

## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Accusation before verification versus verification before accusation | Illustrative | Shows how changing sequence alters what later evidence can plausibly accomplish even if the dossier ends up containing the same items. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** If evidence were rationally processed, order should wash out in the long run; path dependence may reflect only human or bureaucratic irrationality, not a structural feature of inquiry.
- **Response:** The claim is structural because procedures, categories, and admissibility conditions are themselves changed by sequence, so order becomes part of evidentiary content rather than a mere processing error.
