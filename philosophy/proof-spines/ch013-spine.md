# Spine: ch013 — Counterfactual Branching

**Volume:** II: Causal Explosion
**Part:** III. The Combinatorics of Context
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Causal judgment depends on the counterfactual branching functional \(\mathcal B(X)\), which measures the materially distinct outcome spaces opened by plausible interventions and thereby disciplines responsibility by nearby alternatives.

## Depends on
- ch011 — attribution requires mapping a wider causal neighborhood than the salient trigger alone reveals.
- ch012 — the same outcome can arise from multiple structured histories, so comparison among alternatives is unavoidable.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Because causal explanation is difference-making, responsibility begins with intervention contrasts such as \(Y_{do(A=a)}\neq Y_{do(A=a')}\) rather than with the actual sequence alone.
2. Because legal cases involve multiple agents, delay, feedback, strategic adaptation, and omissions, the relevant counterfactuals must be organized through structural causal models and potential outcomes rather than free narrative improvisation.
3. Because the counterfactual branching functional \(\mathcal B(X)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}|\Omega_{do(a)}|\) counts materially distinct outcome spaces under plausible interventions, it measures how widely a case fans out under disciplined branching.
4. Because overdetermination, preemption, and omission all depend on which branches are live and which differences matter, \(\mathcal B(X)\) grounds a non-fantastical comparison class for causal and legal judgment.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** Counterfactuals are unverifiable speculations about unreal worlds, so they cannot do serious evidentiary work. **Response:** The chapter answers that useful counterfactuals are anchored by observed mechanisms and feasible deviations inside the actual world's constraints.
