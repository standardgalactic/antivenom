# Spine: ch049 — Merge, Link, and Unlink

**Volume:** V: Admissibility and Continuation
**Part:** IX. A General Theory of Institutional State Change
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Institutional correction depends on distinguishing merge, link, and unlink, because a false association must be withdrawable without erasing the underlying record.


## Depends on
- **ch047, "Record, Verify, Recognize, Authorize"** — trustworthy state change depends on preserving distinct operations and their provenance.
- **ch048, "Pop, Refuse, Bind, Collapse"** — credible process requires lawful refusal, reversible commitment, and an account of collapse when distinctions are lost.

## Load-bearing steps
1. After the operator algebra of the previous chapter, this one introduces derived relational operations for evidentiary synthesis: merge, link, and unlink.
2. Merge is stronger than link because it fuses files or claims into one operative object, whereas link preserves distinct provenance while making the relation actionable.
3. Unlink is formally crucial because a record may need to be detached from a claim without being deleted; the history of association can remain inspectable even after the association is withdrawn.
4. This yields a non-destructive correction model in which false joins are reversible without pretending they never occurred.
5. The chapter's central inference is that many institutional mistakes are relational-structural errors---bad merges, bad links, missing unlinks---rather than simple deficits of information.


## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Watchlist or entity-resolution systems | Illustrative | Soft links and hard merges have different risk profiles, and false positives require lawful unlinking. |
| Consolidated versus cross-referenced court dockets | Illustrative | Two matters can be related for handling purposes without becoming the same case. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** Modern institutional intelligence comes from integration, so insisting on merge/link distinctions simply preserves wasteful fragmentation. **Response:** Integration helps only when provenance survives and false joins can be undone; otherwise institutions scale their errors along with their data.
