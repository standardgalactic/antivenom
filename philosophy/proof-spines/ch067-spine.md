# Spine: ch067 — Viral Evidence

**Volume:** VI: Adversaries, Platforms, and Automated Judgment
**Part:** XII. Platforms and Automated Publicity
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Viral evidence is governed by the early-interval inequality \(\frac{dV}{dt}\gg\frac{dQ}{dt}\): visibility outruns verification before public judgment reaches saturation.

## Depends on
- ch065 — platforms structure salience and record, so virality already reflects evidentiary architecture rather than neutral circulation.
- ch066 — moderation is compressed adjudication, which means viral material pressures both platforms and publics to judge before full context is available.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Introduce the two time-varying quantities from the outline: visibility \(V(t)\) and verification quality \(Q(t)\).
2. State the decisive early-interval inequality \(\frac{dV}{dt}\gg\frac{dQ}{dt}\), which formalizes the fact that circulation accelerates faster than corroboration.
3. Infer from that rate disparity that public judgment can reach saturation while provenance and context remain underdeveloped.
4. Conclude that \emph{viral evidence} is dangerous not because publicity is worthless, but because visibility dynamics outrun verification dynamics at the moment decisions harden.

## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Covington Catholic video controversy | Illustrative | widely cited example of rapid judgment from partial clips before fuller context emerged |
| Viral false Pentagon-explosion image that moved markets | Illustrative | shows how highly shareable artifacts can outrun verification and create real consequences |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- Demanding friction or delay for viral evidence will mainly protect abusers, because many genuine wrongs become publicly undeniable only when footage spreads fast.
- Response: the chapter defends preserving circulation while attaching uncertainty and provenance cues, not suppressing urgent exposure altogether.
