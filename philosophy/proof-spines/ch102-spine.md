# Spine: ch102 — Rights as Computational Constraints

**Volume:** IX: Institutional Design
**Part:** XVII. Designing for Delayed Truth
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Rights should operate as computational constraints that restrict the permissible action set before institutional optimization begins.

## Depends on
- ch101 (Procedural Regularization) — procedure remains fragile if rights arrive only as end-stage rhetoric rather than as design limits on the process itself.
- ch033 (The Right to Remain Underdescribed) — freedom requires protection against compulsory completeness in dossiers and standardized accounts.
- ch032 (Privacy as Protection Against Model Capture) — privacy partly limits the power to externalize and operationalize someone else's pattern before she can contest it.
- ch042 (Warrants and the Cost of Looking) — legitimate systems make observation and inference costly on purpose before intrusion occurs.
- ch075 (Privacy–Publicity Duality) — privacy and publicity jointly structure what evidence may be gathered and how legitimate scrutiny is preserved.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Because institutions are action-selecting systems, rights matter operationally by restricting the set \(\mathcal A\) of technically possible actions to the permissible subset \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{legal}}=\{a\in\mathcal A:a\models\mathcal R\}\).
2. Because post hoc balancing occurs after collection and inference have already run, rights applied only at the end fail to block many of the harms that architecture can create upstream.
3. Because rights can constrain collection, linkage, retention, use, and intervention, they function as design limits on what the system may optimize over at all.
4. Therefore freedom is protected more reliably when optimization occurs inside the rights-constrained action space rather than across the full technical action space.

## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Data-minimization and purpose-limitation rules in privacy regulation | Illustrative | Exemplifies rights biting at collection and use stages rather than only in downstream remedial balancing. |
| Warrant minimization requirements for digital searches | Illustrative | Shows a legal regime trying to encode ex ante limits on what a lawful search may process. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** Hard constraints make systems too rigid to handle unforeseen contexts where broader collection or inference would avert serious harm.
- **Response:** Constraint architectures can include narrowly specified override paths, but rights cease to be rights if ordinary convenience routinely dissolves them.
