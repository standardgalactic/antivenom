# Spine: ch092 — When Privacy Conceals Domination

**Volume:** VIII: Applications and Case Structures
**Part:** XVI. Failure Modes of the Theory
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Privacy ceases to be a civil shield when domestic, corporate, religious, military, or governmental secrecy prevents evidence from entering public consideration at all and turns opacity into an unreviewable jurisdiction.

## Depends on
- ch091 (When Procedure Protects Power) — formally fair channels can be structured so that complaint never becomes actionable against the strong.
- ch087 (Child Protection and Family Privacy) — protected family space can contain both legitimate intimacy and grave harms that are hard to evidence from outside.
- ch088 (Employment and Institutional Reputation) — confidentiality and reputation management often turn private process into unreviewable institutional memory.
- ch075 (Privacy–Publicity Duality) — privacy and publicity co-produce legitimate scrutiny and neither should be absolutized.
- ch076 (The Freedom Region) — liberty requires protected indeterminacy, but only so long as opacity is not monopolized by those who control dependence.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Secrecy operates differently across households, corporations, churches, military units, and state agencies, but in each setting it can stop evidence from becoming publicly contestable in the first place.
2. The decisive question is not whether a domain calls itself private, but who controls records, testimony, and exit, and whether subordinates can safely resist its confidentiality terms.
3. Once a protected sphere can discipline witnesses, suppress documentation, and route complaints only through itself, privacy begins functioning as an epistemic technology of domination.
4. The theory must therefore permit outside review, protected reporting, and limited disclosure in exactly those cases where internal secrecy would otherwise prevent any public consideration of the evidence.
5. Privacy remains worth defending only when it protects persons from arbitrary exposure without licensing opaque power to govern itself beyond review.

## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Clergy-abuse secrecy regimes within closed religious institutions | Load-bearing | A paradigmatic instance of privacy and internal discipline preventing evidence aggregation and outside review. |
| NDAs and confidential settlements in harassment or retaliation cases | Illustrative | Shows corporate secrecy blocking pattern recognition even when each individual complaint is partly documented. |
| Coercive control in households or hazing secrecy inside military units | Illustrative | Extends the point to domains where control over testimony and exit is itself part of the abuse. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection / failure mode:** Once privacy is treated with suspicion, the state will always find a reason to intrude further into intimate life. **Response:** Unresolved unless intrusion is tightly limited to dependency-marked situations with high review and narrow scope; the chapter rejects sacralization, not privacy itself.
