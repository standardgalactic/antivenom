## Claim
The Spherepop algebra of Pop, Refuse, Bind, and Collapse shows that institutional state change is order-sensitive, because compositions such as \(\mathsf C\circ\mathsf B\) and \(\mathsf B\circ\mathsf C\) generally do not commute.


## Depends on

## Load-bearing steps
1. The chapter begins by typing the Spherepop primitives as operators: Pop introduces a live distinction, Refuse blocks a transition, Bind joins evidence/claim/person/rule/responsibility under a relation, and Collapse resolves an alternative field into an operative determination.
2. Once those operators are explicit, institutional action can be studied compositionally rather than narratively: one asks not only what happened, but in what order the operators fired.
3. The key formal question is whether collapse and binding commute, i.e. whether \(\mathsf C\circ\mathsf B\stackrel{?}{=}\mathsf B\circ\mathsf C\).
4. In legal settings they usually do not: binding evidence to a person before interpretive collapse differs from collapsing a story first and then attaching the person to the settled narrative.
5. The load-bearing result is that order sensitivity is intrinsic to institutional state change, so refusal, binding, and collapse cannot be treated as interchangeable administrative moments.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** These are merely administrative edge cases; the real substance of institutions lies in creating valid outcomes, not in managing reversal or failure. **Response:** Outcomes are trustworthy only if the institution can say when they are not yet binding, when they must be refused, and how they can be lawfully undone.
