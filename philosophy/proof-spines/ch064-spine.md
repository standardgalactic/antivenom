# Spine: ch064 — The Asymmetry of Defensive Paranoia

**Volume:** VI: Adversaries, Platforms, and Automated Judgment
**Part:** XI. Untrustworthy Actors
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Defensive paranoia is politically asymmetric because suspicion can be survival knowledge for the less powerful but a surveillance license for the powerful, depending on who is authorized to act.

## Depends on
- ch059 — adversaries act selectively and adaptively, so defenders face targeted rather than evenly distributed risk.
- ch060 — correlated capture shows that a few compromised nodes can impose system-wide failure, increasing the cost of missed threats.
- ch061 — strategic transparency explains why defenders cannot simply answer asymmetry by exposing every method and threshold.
- ch062 — institutions need protected channels for disclosure because retaliation and concealment raise the cost of missing real warnings.
- ch063 — manufactured uncertainty magnifies the defender's burden by making proof harder precisely where intervention is most urgent.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Distinguish suspicion exercised by the less powerful from suspicion exercised by the powerful, since the same epistemic vocabulary can attach to radically different capacities for action.
2. Show that defensive paranoia can be survival knowledge when people must navigate institutions they cannot inspect, predict, or contest.
3. Show in parallel that the powerful can use the same language of vigilance to authorize surveillance over those who cannot resist.
4. Conclude that the political meaning of paranoia depends on who is empowered to operationalize suspicion, not on the abstract posture alone.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- Security institutions always invoke asymmetry to excuse overreach, so this argument simply gives philosophical cover to permanent emergency powers.
- Response: the chapter makes procedural boundedness the condition of legitimacy; without review, contestability, and audit, the asymmetry argument fails rather than succeeds.
