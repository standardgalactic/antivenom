# Spine: ch023 — Visibility Is Not Knowledge

**Volume:** III: Attention and Publicity
**Part:** V. Publicity Without Comprehension
**Status:** spine

**Foundational chapter** — opens a new Part with no upstream dependency. Prioritize reaching `reviewed` status here before drafting later chapters in this Part in earnest.

## Claim
Knowledge is governed not by visibility alone but by \(K=f(V,I,C,A)\), so increasing volume \(V\) without indexing \(I\), contextual integrity \(C\), and available attention \(A\) can reduce rather than raise knowability.

## Depends on
- ch022 — a just order must balance closure with corrigibility because inquiry often has to stop before uncertainty disappears, so this chapter clarifies how little raw visibility can warrant before institutions close judgment.

## Load-bearing steps
1. The chapter rejects the naive identity \(\text{more recording}=\text{more knowledge}\) and replaces it with the multivariate knowledge relation \(K=f(V,I,C,A)\).
2. In that functional, evidentiary volume \(V\) is only one input alongside indexing quality \(I\), contextual integrity \(C\), and available attention \(A\), so disclosure alone cannot settle relevance or cause.
3. Holding \(I\), \(C\), and \(A\) fixed, raising \(V\) can lower the probability that a relevant trace is found and correctly interpreted.
4. Raw visibility therefore amplifies appearances without determining evidentiary weight, leaving room for overload, manipulation, and false confidence.
5. Institutions need mediating structures that improve \(I\), preserve \(C\), and allocate \(A\) if visible traces are to become contestable knowledge.

## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Police body-camera footage released without corroborating context | Illustrative | Shows that more seeing can still leave causal and normative questions unresolved. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- Objection: broad disclosure is enough because distributed observers will collectively detect what any gatekeeper misses, so the right response to uncertainty is always more visibility.
- Response: more visibility is often necessary, but it is not sufficient; without shared standards for relevance, preservation, and verification, distributed observers inherit the same underdetermination at larger scale.
