# Spine: ch014 — Ecological Judgment

**Volume:** II: Causal Explosion
**Part:** III. The Combinatorics of Context
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Ecological judgment asks for the smallest intelligible scale in the multiscale embedding \(X^{(0)}\subset X^{(1)}\subset\cdots\subset X^{(L)}\), because the causal type of an act depends on its surrounding environment.

## Depends on
- ch011 — causal attribution requires selecting a relevant neighborhood rather than assuming the world announces its own boundaries.
- ch012 — inquiry faces many structured pathways to the same outcome rather than a single transparent history.
- ch013 — comparing those pathways requires disciplined counterfactual branching rather than free fantasy.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Because an apparently local act can change causal type once technical, interpersonal, institutional, and ecological surroundings are included, judgment must model context across levels rather than at one visual scale.
2. Because the multiscale embedding \(X^{(0)}\subset X^{(1)}\subset\cdots\subset X^{(L)}\) orders those surroundings, it gives inquiry a formal way to represent progressively wider environments.
3. Because some explanatory failures come from stopping at too low a level while others come from expanding without bound, the task is to find the smallest \(X^{(\ell)}\) at which the event becomes intelligible.
4. Because heuristics, burdens, privacy rules, and presumptions succeed only relative to the environment they regulate, ecological judgment evaluates decision rules by fit to the relevant scale, not by fantasies of total context or zero context.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** Ecological judgment just baptizes local custom, allowing biased or oppressive practices to excuse themselves as "fit for the environment." **Response:** The chapter's test is comparative and empirical about error patterns, not deferential to whatever a setting currently happens to normalize.
