# Spine: ch004 — Paranoia, Skepticism, and Conspiracy

**Volume:** I: The Epistemology of Suspicion
**Part:** I. The Power of Paranoia
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Paranoia becomes conspiratorial not by positing hidden coordination, but by adopting a self-sealing evidential structure in which \(P(E\mid H)\approx P(E'\mid H)\) even for incompatible observations.

## Depends on
- ch001 — suspicion can be a disciplined epistemic operator rather than a vice.
- ch002 — underdetermination gives genuine reason to resist premature causal closure.
- ch003 — resisting the first available story is often epistemically necessary.

## Load-bearing steps
1. The chapter first refuses the crude rule that any hypothesis involving coordination is conspiratorial, since coordination is ordinary in many institutional settings.
2. Its real criterion is exclusion behavior: a hypothesis becomes pathological when \(P(E\mid H)\approx P(E'\mid H)\) for mutually incompatible observations.
3. This criterion lets the chapter distinguish \textbf{open suspicion}, which names falsifiers and competing explanations, from \textbf{self-sealing suspicion}, which turns absence and counterevidence into further confirmation.
4. The result is a defense of suspicion that remains evidence-responsive rather than allowing hidden-agency explanations to become unfalsifiable closure.

## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Watergate-style investigative suspicion versus totalizing internet conspiracy culture | Illustrative | Contrasts suspicion that remained evidence-responsive with suspicion that feeds on immune-to-refutation narrative closure. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- The distinction between justified suspicion and conspiracy thinking is politically manipulable, because dominant institutions can always call unwelcome dissent "self-sealing" when it threatens them.
- Response: the chapter grounds the distinction in evidential openness and responsiveness to counterevidence, not in whether an authority approves the conclusion.
