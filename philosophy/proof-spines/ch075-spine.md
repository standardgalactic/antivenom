# Spine: ch075 — Privacy–Publicity Duality

**Volume:** VII: Formal Synthesis
**Part:** XIII. A Mathematics of Disciplined Distrust
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Privacy--publicity duality is the paired constraint system $\mathcal O(x)\leq\kappa$ and $\operatorname{Opacity}(g)\leq\rho$, jointly limiting personal observability and hidden official authorization.

## Depends on
- ch073 (Evidentiary Reach) — establishes that evidence is only warranted within a bounded context and should not automatically travel across domains
- ch074 (Update Budgets) — shows that institutional revision must be proportional to the quality and reach of evidence, making the production and contestation conditions of evidence newly central

## Load-bearing steps
1. Let $x$ denote personal information and $g$ institutional action, then formalize privacy as the observability constraint $\mathcal O(x)\leq\kappa$.
2. Formalize publicity as the accountability constraint $\operatorname{Opacity}(g)\leq\rho$, which limits how much official authorization can remain hidden.
3. Show that maximizing privacy alone risks concealed private domination, while maximizing publicity alone risks universal exposure and the destruction of contextual integrity.
4. Treat legitimate institutions as those that satisfy both inequalities together, preserving protected opacity for persons while exposing coercive or classificatory action to challenge.
5. Conclude that freedom lies at neither endpoint because reliable evidence and answerable power are co-produced by the joint maintenance of these two constraints.

## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Secret ballot and public counting | Illustrative | Shows how private formation of judgment and public accountability of outcome can be mutually reinforcing rather than opposed. |
| Protected personal records with reviewable official reasons | Illustrative | Captures the distinction between shielding raw life-data and exposing the state's use of that data to challenge. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** Privacy often shields abuse, corruption, and private domination, so calling it a co-producer of reliable evidence risks disguising secrecy as freedom.
- **Response:** The claim is dual, not absolutist: privacy protects persons from premature evidentiary conversion, while publicity attaches to official reasoning and coercive acts precisely to block unanswerable secrecy.
