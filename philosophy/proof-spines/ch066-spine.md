# Spine: ch066 — Moderation as Compressed Adjudication

**Volume:** VI: Adversaries, Platforms, and Automated Judgment
**Part:** XII. Platforms and Automated Publicity
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Moderation is compressed adjudication implemented through rules, classifiers, queues, and confidence thresholds under constrained attention and asymmetric error costs.

## Depends on
- ch065 — platforms are evidentiary institutions whose architectural choices determine what material is seen, retained, and acted on.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Define moderation at scale by the outline's technical substitutes for individualized adjudication: rules, classifiers, queues, and confidence thresholds.
2. Place those substitutes under the two governing constraints named in the outline---constrained attention and asymmetric error costs---so that the moderation problem is formally one of scarce review capacity.
3. Show that queue order, classifier output, and threshold choice already decide what receives investigation and what kinds of mistakes the system is built to tolerate.
4. Conclude that moderation is compressed adjudication because architectural settings, not full case-by-case hearings, carry the burden of public judgment.

## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Facebook Oversight Board and limited appeals structure | Illustrative | highlights the gap between mass moderation and selective quasi-appellate review |
| YouTube automated copyright strikes | Illustrative | shows rule application and sanction under scale-induced procedural compression |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- Calling moderation "adjudication" falsely legalizes private curation; platforms are simply enforcing house rules in voluntary spaces.
- Response: the label tracks function, not formal state status: when rule application under scarce process imposes material sanctions, adjudicative problems already exist.
