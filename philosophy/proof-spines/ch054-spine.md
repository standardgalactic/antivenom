# Spine: ch054 — The Press and the Court

**Volume:** V: Admissibility and Continuation
**Part:** X. Public Institutions as Epistemic Ecologies
**Status:** spine

## Claim
The press and the court must remain distinct because they optimize different loss functions and time horizons: timely public relevance versus procedurally warranted determination.


## Depends on
- **ch053, "Separation of Powers"** — reliable public action depends on institutional nonidentity across procedures, tempos, and standards of judgment.
- **ch050, "The Admissibility Boundary"** — coercive forums preserve fairness by narrowing which appearances may properly enter judgment.

## Load-bearing steps
1. The chapter begins from asymmetric optimization: the press is organized around timely public relevance, while the court is organized around procedurally warranted determination.
2. Those differing loss functions imply different time horizons, thresholds, and tolerances for incompleteness even when both institutions address the same accusation.
3. Public urgency can therefore outrun legal sufficiency, just as judicial restraint can be misread as indifference to fact.
4. The productive relation is not identity but tension: journalism expands the visible field, whereas adjudication narrows it to what may bear coercive consequence.
5. The conclusion is that exposure and sanction must remain institutionally distinct, because collapsing them imports the wrong error costs into both domains.


## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Investigative reporting that precedes later litigation or prosecution | Illustrative | Public exposure can widen the field of attention before a court is entitled to impose sanctions. |
| Defamation or contempt disputes arising from public allegations | Illustrative | Courts revisit already public claims under a radically narrower decision standard. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** In high-stakes matters, conflicting narratives from press and courts only confuse the public, so the two should converge on a common fact-finding model. **Response:** Their divergence is precisely what keeps exposure and sanction from inheriting the same error costs and the same temptations.
