# Spine: ch026 — Flooding, Noise, and Plausible Distraction

**Volume:** III: Attention and Publicity
**Part:** V. Publicity Without Comprehension
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Flooding works by driving effective discoverability toward \(D \propto S/(S+N)\), so noise can neutralize evidence without censoring any datum.

## Depends on
- ch023 — publicity does not amount to knowledge without structures that stabilize relevance and inference.
- ch024 — overload can itself operate as opacity.
- ch025 — attention is a scarce public resource whose allocation shapes what can be known.

## Load-bearing steps
1. The chapter treats censorship and flooding as dual operations: censorship shrinks accessible signal, whereas flooding leaves signal present while enlarging distracting mass.
2. With relevant signal mass \(S\), distracting mass \(N\), and bounded attention, effective discoverability scales as \(D \propto S/(S+N)\).
3. As \(N\) rises, the same finite attention budget must be spent distinguishing real evidence from spam, duplication, performative transparency, and adversarial disclosure.
4. Because no single item need be hidden, flooding preserves deniability while making archives technically accessible yet practically unusable.
5. The strategic aim is therefore not rebuttal but the destruction of sustained coherence: noise keeps inquiry from remaining stable long enough to bite.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- Objection: competent investigators should be able to filter noise, so flooding mainly exposes weakness in audiences rather than a distinctive tactic of power.
- Response: filtering itself consumes scarce capacity and coordination; flooding is potent precisely because it raises verification costs systemically even for competent investigators.
