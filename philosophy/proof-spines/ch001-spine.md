## Claim
Suspicion is formalized by the operator \(\Pi:\mathcal E\to 2^{\mathcal H}\), which preserves causally possible histories not yet excluded by the evidence and blocks premature closure from possibility to authorization; disciplined paranoia (the book's subject) is distinguished from naivety and from pathological paranoia by three constraints on how \(\Pi(E)\) may be used.

## Load-bearing steps
1. The chapter first fixes a three-state trichotomy — naivety (presumed sufficiency), disciplined paranoia (unrepresented causes remain possible), pathological paranoia (every observation confirms hostile agency) — so that "paranoia" is never used loosely for the rest of the book.
2. It then defines suspicion with the operator \(\Pi:\mathcal E\to 2^{\mathcal H}\), so the first formal task is to distinguish preserving a live hypothesis set from selecting a single favored history.
3. It introduces \textbf{premature closure} as a selection map \(C_\tau:\Pi(E_\tau)\to \widehat H_\tau\) that collapses inquiry before the relevant verification procedures have been completed.
4. Disciplined paranoia is then characterized as three constraints on the use of \(\Pi(E)\): specifying what evidence would weaken suspicion, assigning unequal plausibility across the members of \(\Pi(E)\), and forbidding action on mere membership in \(\Pi(E)\); pathological paranoia is the specific failure of the latter two constraints.
5. The distinction \(\text{suspicion}\neq\text{belief}\neq\text{proof}\neq\text{authorization}\), sharpened by the non-implication chain \(\operatorname{Possible}(H)\centernot\Rightarrow\operatorname{Probable}(H)\centernot\Rightarrow\operatorname{Proven}(H)\centernot\Rightarrow\operatorname{Actionable}(H)\), shows why disciplined suspicion can preserve possibilities without licensing equal belief or immediate action.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- If suspicion is normalized whenever appearances admit multiple causes, ordinary inquiry will collapse into chronic hesitation, because most evidence underdetermines more than one explanation.
- Response: the chapter's norms limit suspicion to proportionate, revisable withholding, so it blocks premature certainty without forbidding action or all-confidence judgments.
