# Spine: ch104 — Institutions That Can Say “Not Yet”

**Volume:** IX: Institutional Design
**Part:** XVII. Designing for Delayed Truth
**Status:** spine

## Claim
A mature institution needs explicit states of “not yet” so incomplete evidence can delay closure without being confused with denial, refusal, or final judgment.

## Depends on
- ch097 (Reopenable Records) — institutions must preserve revisable memory rather than encode first-pass decisions as absolute.
- ch098 (Layered Verdicts) — action can be graded by confidence and consequence instead of collapsed into a single binary closure.
- ch099 (Bounded Transparency) — challenge-enabling disclosure must be distinguished from exposure that destroys the goods scrutiny is meant to protect.
- ch100 (Attention-Aware Archives) — evidence must remain usable within real cognitive limits if delay is to produce better judgment rather than mere accumulation.
- ch101 (Procedural Regularization) — principled delay requires ex ante sequences for notice, response, burden allocation, and review.
- ch102 (Rights as Computational Constraints) — some moves must be blocked in advance so that waiting does not occur inside an already rights-violating machinery.
- ch103 (Audit Without Total Surveillance) — the discipline of delay must itself be reviewable without collapsing into panoptic oversight.
- ch046 (Delay, Liberty, and the Protected Interval) — some delays protect the interval in which accusation can still be tested before power hardens into fact.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Because incomplete evidence is neither a denial nor a vindication, institutions need explicit procedural states that distinguish delay from refusal, refusal from denial, and provisional action from final judgment.
2. Because postponement can be responsible rather than evasive, a “not yet” state should preserve the question, mark the evidentiary deficiency, and specify what would permit reconsideration.
3. Because delay can also become abuse, those states need deadlines and scheduled reconsideration rather than indefinite suspension.
4. Therefore temporal discipline is achieved not by forcing closure, but by making unfinished judgment legible and governable.

## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Stay of execution or sentence pending new forensic review | Illustrative | Exemplifies delay justified because later evidence could make irreversible state action wrongful. |
| Interim injunction preserving the status quo while merits are tested | Illustrative | Shows how institutions can act provisionally to prevent irreversible harm without pretending final truth is already in hand. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** A culture of "not yet" invites timidity and bureaucratic drift, letting urgent harms continue while institutions congratulate themselves for epistemic humility.
- **Response:** The chapter must pair postponement with thresholds, interim protections, and criteria for when delay itself becomes wrongful.
