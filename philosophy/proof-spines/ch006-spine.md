# Spine: ch006 — The Event Is Not the Record

**Volume:** I: The Epistemology of Suspicion
**Part:** II. Events, Records, and Reconstructions
**Status:** spine

**Foundational chapter** — opens a new Part with no upstream dependency. Prioritize reaching `reviewed` status here before drafting later chapters in this Part in earnest.

## Claim
An event is not its record because evidence passes through the transformation chain \(X\xrightarrow{\tau}T\xrightarrow{\kappa}R\xrightarrow{\mu}M\), so recording, retrieval, and recognition are distinct achievements.

## Depends on
- ch001 — suspicion should remain proportionate and attentive to alternative mechanisms rather than collapse record and explanation.
- ch002 — traces underdetermine the hidden processes that produced them, so a record cannot authenticate its own causal history.
- ch005 — distrust toward systems and records can be justified without first proving bad faith by a recorder or custodian.

## Load-bearing steps
1. The chapter formalizes the ontology \(X\xrightarrow{\tau}T\xrightarrow{\kappa}R\xrightarrow{\mu}M\), separating event, trace, record, and representation rather than letting “the record” stand in for all four.
2. Because each map can lose information or impose new structure, evidential evaluation must attend to where in the chain a feature entered, disappeared, or was reformatted.
3. The non-implication chain \(\operatorname{Recorded}(x)\centernot\Rightarrow\operatorname{Retrievable}(x)\centernot\Rightarrow\operatorname{Recognized}(x)\) shows why preservation, access, and comprehension are distinct achievements.
4. This yields the chapter's core discipline: documentary survival has evidential value only once the mediations between event and representation are kept explicit.

## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| A medical chart entry versus the clinical encounter | Illustrative | Shows that the chart is a later formatted trace of a richer event. |
| Security camera footage with missing angles or retention gaps | Illustrative | Illustrates how noncapture and selective preservation create absences without implying nonoccurrence. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- If records are treated as merely constructed traces, then documentary evidence loses the authority needed for administration, law, and history, leaving only speculative mistrust.
- Response: the chapter denies transparency, not evidential value; records remain indispensable evidence precisely once their mode of construction is made part of the evaluation.
