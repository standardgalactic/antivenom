# Spine: ch098 — Layered Verdicts

**Volume:** IX: Institutional Design
**Part:** XVII. Designing for Delayed Truth
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Institutions should issue layered, typed verdicts without collapsing “not proven” into either falsity or truth by implication.

## Depends on
- ch097 (Reopenable Records) — if the record remains corrigible, judgments must specify what is settled for now and what remains open to revision.
- ch036 (Burdens and Standards of Proof) — different thresholds express graded permissions to act under uncertainty rather than all-or-nothing access to truth.
- ch040 (Appeal and Reversible Judgment) — a judgment can be authoritative in the present without being epistemically final.
- ch083 (Criminal Investigation) — leads, search grounds, arrest grounds, and conviction each license different updates and different consequences.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Because many inquiries end in structured uncertainty, institutions need typed determinations such as substantiated, unsubstantiated, disproven, unresolved, procedurally barred, and outside jurisdiction.
2. Because each status does different work, a layered verdict must keep those categories distinct instead of translating them back into a crude true/false binary.
3. Because “not proven” is neither falsity nor covert confirmation, the system must prevent that status from silently mutating into either “false” or “probably true.”
4. Therefore verdict design should encode what sort of closure has been reached, for what purpose, and with what remainder of uncertainty still attached.

## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Criminal process distinction among probable cause, conviction, and sentencing | Load-bearing | Supplies the clearest existing example of different thresholds attached to different legal consequences. |
| Intelligence warning versus public attribution of atrocity | Illustrative | Shows how the same underlying evidence may justify precaution earlier than punishment or definitive public judgment. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** Layering verdicts muddies institutional communication; citizens need one clear answer, not technical gradations that officials can manipulate.
- **Response:** The manipulation risk is greater when different levels are hidden inside one overconfident verdict, since layering makes the scope of each claim publicly contestable.
