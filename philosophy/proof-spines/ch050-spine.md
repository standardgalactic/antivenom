# Spine: ch050 — The Admissibility Boundary

**Volume:** V: Admissibility and Continuation
**Part:** IX. A General Theory of Institutional State Change
**Status:** spine

## Claim
The \textbf{admissibility boundary} \(\partial\mathcal D_c\) separates claims in \(\Omega\) that a forum may presently process from those that remain contested, incomplete, or evidentially immature.


## Depends on
- **ch047, "Record, Verify, Recognize, Authorize"** — appearances must pass through distinct operations before they can take public effect.
- **ch048, "Pop, Refuse, Bind, Collapse"** — refusal and binding show that entry into process is itself a governed decision, not a neutral default.
- **ch049, "Merge, Link, and Unlink"** — relational structure determines whether an appearance arrives in a form the institution can responsibly handle.

## Load-bearing steps
1. The chapter formalizes forum fitness by letting \(\Omega\) be the space of conceivable claims and \(\mathcal D_c\subseteq\Omega\) the subset admissible in context \(c\).
2. The operative site is the boundary \(\partial\mathcal D_c\), where claims are contested, incomplete, or still dependent on further evidence.
3. Admissibility is therefore not a truth predicate but a context-indexed decision about whether a claim is ready to bear consequence in this forum.
4. Because many disputes concern whether a claim should cross into \(\mathcal D_c\), institutional conflict often concerns movement of the boundary more than disagreement over the claim's ultimate truth-value.
5. The chapter thus makes explicit that exclusion, supplementation, and boundary-shifting are constitutive parts of disciplined reasoning rather than mere preliminaries.


## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Exclusion of hearsay or unduly prejudicial evidence | Illustrative | A court may treat material as real yet presently unfit for coercive judgment. |
| Input validation in safety-critical software | Illustrative | Systems preserve intelligibility by refusing malformed input before downstream processing. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** Admissibility rules mainly shield institutions from inconvenient truths, so exclusion is just censorship under procedural cover. **Response:** The chapter's answer is that admissibility concerns present forum-competence and must itself be contestable, not that exclusion is always innocent.
