## Claim
Institutional belief is modeled on a belief-state manifold $\mathcal M$ with admissible motion constrained by $\dot\Theta_t\in\mathcal D_{\Theta_t}\subseteq T_{\Theta_t}\mathcal M$.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Model institutional states as points $\Theta_t\in\mathcal M$, turning revision into movement through a structured space rather than into isolated proposition replacement.
2. Identify local possible motions with the tangent space $T_{\Theta_t}\mathcal M$, so nearby reinterpretations and larger reorganizations can be distinguished geometrically.
3. Constrain actual institutional movement by the distribution $\mathcal D_{\Theta_t}\subseteq T_{\Theta_t}\mathcal M$, encoding legal and evidentiary admissibility directly in the geometry.
4. Use the difference between the full tangent space and the admissible distribution to explain why some imaginable updates are blocked, forbidden, or normatively dangerous even when conceptually describable.
5. Treat this manifold-plus-distribution structure as the formal stage on which later chapters analyze nonintegrability, curvature, and holonomy.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** The manifold picture falsely smooths what are really discrete judgments, categorical breaks, and political decisions, importing geometric elegance at the cost of realism.
- **Response:** The model is local and structural: it represents neighborhoods, discontinuities, and blocked transitions precisely so that rupture and singularity can be described rather than ignored.
