# Spine: ch033 — The Right to Remain Underdescribed

**Volume:** III: Attention and Publicity
**Part:** VI. Privacy as Boundary and Capacity
**Status:** spine

## Claim
The right to remain \textbf{underdescribed} blocks the inference-to-use move: the fact that a pattern can be extracted from records does not entail that institutions may demand exhaustive explanation or operationalize that pattern.

## Depends on
- ch029 — privacy is a condition of agency and role-differentiated life, not just secrecy.
- ch030 — assembled traces can reveal more than any isolated disclosure.
- ch031 — what may be demanded or transmitted depends on contextual role and purpose.
- ch032 — model capture turns exhaustive legibility into a tool of unilateral prediction and control.

## Load-bearing steps
1. The chapter introduces underdescription as a political good opposed to the demand that persons be perfectly resolved into predictive objects.
2. Extensive records make anomaly-explanations technically demandable, but the chapter denies that technical demandability creates legitimate authority to require exhaustive self-accounting.
3. This is not a right to deceive: it limits the move from what can be inferred to what may be institutionally used or demanded.
4. The threat is sharpened by model capture, because compulsory completeness feeds administratively durable dossiers and narrows revisable self-presentation.
5. Freedom therefore requires space for partial opacity, experimentation, and context-specific disclosure rather than permanent exhaustive legibility.

## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Job or school intake systems demanding exhaustive personal history | Illustrative | Shows how “just more context” can become coercive completeness. |
| Social-credit-style or unified reputation systems | Illustrative | Makes the political danger of stable total legibility vivid. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- Objection: institutions need rich information to allocate risk and opportunity fairly, so a right to remain underdescribed entrenches arbitrariness and hidden bias.
- Response: legitimate institutions may demand necessity-based, role-specific information, but compulsory exhaustiveness overshoots coordination needs and converts fairness into domination by dossier.
