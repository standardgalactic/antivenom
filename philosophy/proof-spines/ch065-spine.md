# Spine: ch065 — Platforms as Evidentiary Institutions

**Volume:** VI: Adversaries, Platforms, and Automated Judgment
**Part:** XII. Platforms and Automated Publicity
**Status:** spine

**Foundational chapter** — opens a new Part with no upstream dependency. Prioritize reaching `reviewed` status here before drafting later chapters in this Part in earnest.

## Claim
Platforms are evidentiary institutions because collection, ranking, visibility control, categorization, and retention or erasure decisions determine what later counts as usable evidence.

## Depends on
- ch059 — adversarial agency must be modeled in terms of who can benefit, intervene, persist, and hide, which platforms reorganize at scale.
- ch060 — apparent plurality can mask correlated capture, a risk intensified when many public actors depend on the same platform infrastructures.
- ch061 — visibility matters only when it enables audit rather than theater, which is why platform-controlled transparency must be analyzed structurally.
- ch062 — protected or anonymous disclosures need channels and records whose design affects whether truth can safely surface and be checked.
- ch063 — manufactured uncertainty often works by flooding or fragmenting records, making control over salience and preservation evidentially decisive.
- ch064 — defensive paranoia is justified only within reviewable procedures, a requirement complicated when platforms privately govern what evidence survives and circulates.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Enumerate the platform functions named in the outline---record collection, claim ranking, visibility control, category enforcement, and preservation or erasure of histories---as evidentiary operations.
2. Show that these operations shape salience, chain of custody, and retrievability before downstream courts, journalists, or publics ever see the material.
3. Note that the functions are quasi-legal because they govern what evidence survives and circulates without necessarily carrying corresponding procedural safeguards.
4. Conclude that platforms should be analyzed as evidentiary institutions rather than described as neutral hosts with incidental policy choices.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- Platforms merely host material generated by users; courts, journalists, and publics remain the real evidentiary institutions, so this chapter overstates private-platform power.
- Response: downstream institutions judge only what platform architectures make visible, preservable, and retrievable in the first place.
