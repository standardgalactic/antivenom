# Spine: ch028 — Collective Investigation

**Volume:** III: Attention and Publicity
**Part:** V. Publicity Without Comprehension
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Collective investigation improves knowledge only to the extent that the effective number of independent investigators \(n_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{n}{1+(n-1)\rho}\) stays high, since correlated error can turn crowds into a single amplified mistake.

## Depends on
- ch023 — exposure becomes knowledge only through mediating standards and contestable inference.
- ch024 — evidentiary abundance overwhelms lone investigators unless records are organized.
- ch025 — public attention is a scarce resource that must be institutionally allocated.
- ch026 — noise can disable inquiry by exhausting that scarce attention.
- ch027 — algorithmic systems prestructure which materials become salient in the first place.

## Load-bearing steps
1. The chapter models collective investigation as distributed search with shared priors and non-independent errors rather than as a mere increase in head count.
2. Its key quantity is the effective number of independent investigators, \(n_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{n}{1+(n-1)\rho}\), where \(\rho\) measures average error correlation.
3. As \(\rho\) rises, crowds become vulnerable to copied misreadings, false identification, harassment, and narrative cascades even when \(n\) is large.
4. Organized inquiry creates value by lowering \(\rho\) through role differentiation, adversarial checking, preserved records, and explicit standards for escalating from clue to claim.
5. Publicity becomes knowledge only when institutions convert nominally many observers into genuinely semi-independent checks rather than one synchronized cascade.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- Objection: collective investigation simply reinstates gatekeepers, so the chapter ends by replacing publicity with a new authority class.
- Response: some delegation is unavoidable, but the relevant institutions earn authority only by preserving records, exposing inference, and remaining open to adversarial challenge.
