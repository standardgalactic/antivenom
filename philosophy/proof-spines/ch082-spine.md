# Spine: ch082 — Reknotting After Error

**Volume:** VII: Formal Synthesis
**Part:** XIV. Dynamics, Geometry, and Holonomy
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Reknotting after error is a constrained transformation of the dependency graph that removes dependence on a false judgment while preserving valid downstream relations.

## Depends on
- ch081 (Constraint Relaxation) — shows how loosening constraints can both surface truth and deform the conditions of answerable judgment
- ch079 (Curvature of Reinterpretation) — explains why error correction reshapes surrounding judgments rather than simply flipping one conclusion
- ch080 (RSVP Fields of Public Interpretation) — identifies the ambient pressures that any repair must account for if it is not to reproduce the same distortions

## Load-bearing steps
1. Start from the fact that a false judgment propagates into records, reputations, precedents, and downstream decisions rather than remaining a single isolated proposition.
2. Model repair as a constrained transformation of the dependency graph, targeting the edges and inheritances that derive their authority from the false judgment.
3. Preserve valid subsequent relations where their support can be rebuilt independently, rather than treating every downstream node as equally contaminated.
4. Remove dependencies that cannot survive without the bad judgment, so correction undoes illegitimate propagation instead of merely relabeling the original verdict.
5. Conclude that successful repair is reknotting: neither nostalgic restoration nor total reset, but a publicly justified reweaving of institutional dependencies.

## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Institutional reform after a wrongful-conviction scandal | Illustrative | Shows why credible repair requires redesign of evidentiary and procedural links rather than mere restoration of pre-scandal trust. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** Institutions rarely learn in the orderly way this image suggests; "reknotting" risks romanticizing repair when accumulated error usually leaves only damage and improvised patchwork.
- **Response:** The claim is conditional and normative: when repair is possible, it requires remembered redesign rather than nostalgic reset or permanent emergency.
