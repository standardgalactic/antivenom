# Spine: ch068 — The Permanent Record and the Mutable Person

**Volume:** VI: Adversaries, Platforms, and Automated Judgment
**Part:** XII. Platforms and Automated Publicity
**Status:** spine

## Claim
The permanent record becomes unstable when human change \(X(t)\) is judged through a frozen representation \(R(t_0)\), so an accurate archive can become contextually false in present use.

## Depends on
- ch065 — platforms act as evidentiary institutions by preserving, indexing, and resurfacing records over time.
- ch066 — compressed moderation and platform governance impose consequences even when individualized review of a person's development is impossible.
- ch067 — viral publicity often creates the durable artifacts that later harden into decontextualized reputational records.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Model the person as a changing trajectory \(X(t)\) and the archive as a frozen representation \(R(t_0)\) fixed at an earlier moment.
2. Show that automated retrieval repeatedly re-presents \(R(t_0)\) in later contexts, effectively collapsing biographical time.
3. Distinguish factual accuracy about \(t_0\) from contextual truth about the later person described by \(X(t)\), allowing an accurate record to become misleading in present use.
4. Conclude that just treatment of durable records must attend to temporal fit between \(X(t)\) and \(R(t_0)\), not to archival truth alone.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- Wrongdoers should not escape the consequences of documented acts just because time passes; making records harder to find invites revisionism and selective amnesia.
- Response: the chapter preserves retention for accountability while denying that unlimited searchability and decontextualized recirculation are the same thing as justice.
