# Spine: ch002 — Why Appearances Underdetermine Causes

**Volume:** I: The Epistemology of Suspicion
**Part:** I. The Power of Paranoia
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Appearances underdetermine causes because observed data arise through the channel \(Y_t=\mathcal O(X_t,\eta_t)\), so the same surface evidence can remain compatible with multiple latent causal states.

## Depends on
- ch001 — suspicion is a disciplined response to appearances that admit more than one possible cause.

## Load-bearing steps
1. The chapter models appearance as \(Y_t=\mathcal O(X_t,\eta_t)\), making explicit that evidence arrives through an observation channel rather than as unmediated access to \(X_t\).
2. Because \(\eta_t\) includes noise, occlusion, selection, framing, compression, and measurement error, distinct latent states can map to the same appearance.
3. The non-identifiability condition \(\mathcal O(X_1,\eta_1)=\mathcal O(X_2,\eta_2)\) with \(X_1\neq X_2\) grounds the claim that authentic records can remain causally indecisive.
4. The chapter then differentiates falsified, incomplete, decontextualized, and causally insufficient evidence so that underdetermination is not confused with fakery alone.

## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Fever as a symptom with many etiologies | Illustrative | Distinguishes observable presentation from the many mechanisms that can generate it. |
| A server outage alert with multiple possible root causes | Illustrative | Gives a technical analogue in which the same surface signal supports rival causal stories. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- In everyday life appearances are usually reliable enough, so calling underdetermination the normal condition of inquiry exaggerates a technical problem into a general epistemology.
- Response: reliability for ordinary action does not show that a surface uniquely identifies its cause; the chapter targets explanatory entitlement, not practical usefulness.
