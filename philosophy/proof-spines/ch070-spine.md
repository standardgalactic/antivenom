# Spine: ch070 — Synthetic Evidence and Provenance Failure

**Volume:** VI: Adversaries, Platforms, and Automated Judgment
**Part:** XII. Platforms and Automated Publicity
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Synthetic evidence shifts trust from artifacts to layered provenance systems---cryptographic provenance, sensor authentication, chain of custody, watermarking, and model-based detection---without promising any purely technical guarantee.

## Depends on
- ch065 — platforms are evidentiary institutions, so provenance depends on how records, metadata, and custody are maintained within their infrastructures.
- ch066 — compressed adjudication magnifies the danger of acting on artifacts whose origins cannot be adequately checked in time.
- ch067 — viral spread makes unverified synthetic artifacts consequential before slower verification can catch up.
- ch068 — permanent digital archives preserve both authentic and synthetic material in ways that later detach them from context.
- ch069 — algorithmic accusation becomes especially dangerous when predictive systems ingest or prioritize artifacts whose provenance has failed.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Define the problem as provenance failure: generative media makes fabrication, remixing, and origin-stripping cheap enough that artifact-level trust alone no longer suffices.
2. Enumerate the outline's layered responses---cryptographic provenance, sensor authentication, chain of custody, watermarking, and model-based detection---as partial supports for evidentiary confidence.
3. Note that each support is limited, so the chapter rejects the fantasy of a purely technical guarantee even while defending technical infrastructure as necessary.
4. Conclude that synthetic evidence relocates trust from isolated appearances to auditable provenance pathways whose strengths and failures remain contestable.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- Provenance systems can be gamed, captured, or excluded too, so shifting trust from artifacts to provenance merely relocates the same epistemic problem.
- Response: the chapter grants that no provenance regime is foolproof; its claim is that auditable chains make error and contestation tractable in ways isolated artifacts no longer do.
