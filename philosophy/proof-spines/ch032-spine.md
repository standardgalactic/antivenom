# Spine: ch032 — Privacy as Protection Against Model Capture

**Volume:** III: Attention and Publicity
**Part:** VI. Privacy as Boundary and Capacity
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Privacy guards against \textbf{model capture}, the condition in which \(M_t(x) \to A_t(x)\) without an adequate route back from the represented person to the operative model.

## Depends on
- ch029 — privacy protects boundaries required for agency rather than merely hiding secrets.
- ch030 — composition and accumulation create new forms of revelation beyond any one datum.
- ch031 — legitimacy of information flow depends on context, role, purpose, and transmission constraints.

## Load-bearing steps
1. The chapter defines model capture through the transition \(M_t(x) \to A_t(x)\), where a model of person \(x\) directly governs institutional action at time \(t\).
2. Cross-context traces are pooled and stabilized into \(M_t\), turning observations from many settings into a durable predictive representation.
3. The distinctive harm appears when action \(A_t(x)\) is driven by that representation across employment, insurance, credit, mobility, reputation, or legal scrutiny.
4. If the represented person lacks an adequate route back to inspect, correct, or escape \(M_t\), suspicion becomes effectively unilateral and administratively entrenched.
5. Privacy must therefore limit the capture, refinement, and deployment of person-models, not merely isolated acts of observation.

## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Data-broker and ad-tech profiles assembled across apps and sites | Illustrative | Shows traces becoming a portable model owned by third parties. |
| Hiring, credit, or risk-scoring systems fed by cross-context behavioral data | Illustrative | Makes operational deployment of the model visible. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- Objection: accurate models improve personalization and efficient allocation, so limiting them sacrifices social value merely to preserve discomfort with prediction.
- Response: efficiency does not answer the asymmetry of unilateral modeling; the problem is the uncontestable capture and deployment of a person-pattern as someone else’s instrument.
