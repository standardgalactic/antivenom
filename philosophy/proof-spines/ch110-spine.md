# Spine: ch110 — The Freedom Created by Law

**Volume:** X: Normative Conclusions
**Part:** XVIII. Freedom Under Causal Opacity
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Law creates freedom by forcing records, testimony, measurements, classifications, and arguments to pass through contestable procedure before they become coercive facts.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Because no institution receives innocence or guilt directly from the world, it must act through records, testimony, measurements, classifications, and arguments.
2. Because freedom depends on those representations not becoming coercive facts too quickly, hearings, proof, and delay are enabling frictions rather than mere obstacles.
3. Because legal procedure is part of the machinery that produces action-authorizing knowledge, contestable form stands between suspicion, evidence, fact, and force.
4. Therefore law creates freedom by maintaining the intervals named in the closing formulation: paranoia, privacy, publicity, evidence, procedure, and freedom are linked by the gaps law keeps open.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** Law often stabilizes hierarchy and gives domination a more durable grammar, so calling law the creator of freedom mistakes a coercive instrument for its opposite.
- **Response:** The claim is conditional and architectural: law creates freedom only when it binds power to revisable forms that protect opacity, contestation, and bounded inference rather than freezing domination into procedure.
