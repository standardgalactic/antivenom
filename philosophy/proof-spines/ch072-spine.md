# Spine: ch072 — Premature-Collapse Loss

**Volume:** VII: Formal Synthesis
**Part:** XIII. A Mathematics of Disciplined Distrust
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Premature-collapse loss is the functional $L_{\mathrm{collapse}}(\tau)=C(\widehat H_\tau,H^\ast)+\alpha A(\tau)+\beta R(\tau)$, which prices not only inferential error but the action and reversal costs of committing too early.

## Depends on
- ch071 (The Paranoia Functional) — supplies the baseline measure on which preserving unresolved plurality counts as epistemically responsible

## Load-bearing steps
1. Let $\tau$ denote the time of institutional commitment, $\widehat H_\tau$ the history endorsed at $\tau$, and $H^\ast$ the eventual best-supported history.
2. Define the inferential component $C(\widehat H_\tau,H^\ast)$ to measure the epistemic distance between premature commitment and later best support.
3. Add $\alpha A(\tau)$ to capture the harms authorized by acting on the mistaken commitment, and add $\beta R(\tau)$ to capture the cost of reversing a judgment once it has become institutionally entrenched.
4. Show that premature collapse is therefore a compound loss, not a mere confidence error: it bundles mistaken inference, downstream action, and repair burden.
5. Use $L_{\mathrm{collapse}}(\tau)$ to distinguish productive decisiveness from the systematically costly haste that later chapters constrain through reach and update budgets.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** Delay and indecision also cause real harm, so privileging the losses of premature closure risks romanticizing hesitation and disabling timely action.
- **Response:** The claim is not that all delay is good, but that premature closure imposes a distinctive and often undercounted class of irreversible harms that must be balanced against action costs.
