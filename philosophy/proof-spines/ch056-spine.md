# Spine: ch056 — Archives and Institutional Memory

**Volume:** V: Admissibility and Continuation
**Part:** X. Public Institutions as Epistemic Ecologies
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Archives are future-facing admissibility structures, \(\mathcal A_t\to\{\text{questions answerable at }t+\Delta\}\), so selection, retention, indexing, and degradation directly shape institutional memory.


## Depends on
- **ch047, "Record, Verify, Recognize, Authorize"** — later accountability presupposes records that remain distinguishable from their verification and later use.
- **ch053, "Separation of Powers"** — public knowledge is distributed across institutions and therefore requires durable media through which different offices can re-test one another.
- **ch055, "Science and Adjudication"** — expert-informed judgment still needs preserved traces that later actors can revisit, compare, and challenge.

## Load-bearing steps
1. The chapter defines archives operationally through selection, retention, indexing, and degradation rather than as neutral storage.
2. Its formal image is future-facing: \(\mathcal A_t\longrightarrow\{\text{questions answerable at }t+\Delta\}\).
3. What an institution can later reconstruct depends on what the archive kept salient, what it misfiled, and what it allowed to decay or disappear.
4. Archive management therefore changes future admissibility: preservation broadens later question-space, while omission and degradation narrow it.
5. The chapter's key conclusion is that institutional memory is an engineered capacity for future reconstruction, not a passive pile of leftovers from past action.


## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Land registries and chain-of-title systems | Illustrative | Durable indexing is what makes later recognition and dispute resolution possible. |
| Missing, destroyed, or inaccessible government records | Illustrative | Loss itself changes what future investigators, courts, or publics can responsibly know. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** In a digital age, archives are cheap passive storage, so institutional accountability depends mainly on current decision-makers rather than recordkeeping infrastructures. **Response:** Digital abundance changes storage costs, not the politics of indexing, preservation, deletion, authenticity, and access that determine what can still be checked.
