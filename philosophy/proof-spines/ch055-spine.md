# Spine: ch055 — Science and Adjudication

**Volume:** V: Admissibility and Continuation
**Part:** X. Public Institutions as Epistemic Ecologies
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Science and adjudication must translate between revisable inquiry and finite closure, distinguishing replication, peer review, evidentiary hearings, precedent, and final judgment.


## Depends on
- **ch053, "Separation of Powers"** — reliable institutions preserve nonidentity across forms of inquiry and judgment rather than collapsing authority into one line.
- **ch054, "The Press and the Court"** — public epistemic ecologies work by assigning different institutions different thresholds for surfacing and settling claims.
- **ch050, "The Admissibility Boundary"** — expert material must be screened for forum fitness rather than treated as self-applying truth.

## Load-bearing steps
1. The chapter's first distinction is temporal: scientific conclusions remain revisable, whereas adjudication often must terminate uncertainty in a particular case.
2. It then separates the practices that structure those domains---replication and peer review in science; evidentiary hearings, precedent, and final judgment in law.
3. Because those practices solve different problems, scientific uncertainty cannot simply be imported into legal decision without translation.
4. Conversely, legal demands for binary resolution can distort scientific evidence by forcing provisional models into yes/no verdict forms they were not built to bear.
5. The chapter's governing claim is thus translational: responsible institutions neither collapse science into law nor ignore science, but mediate between revisable inquiry and finite closure.


## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Daubert/Frye treatment of expert testimony | Illustrative | Courts do not absorb science whole; they construct institutional gates for using it in adjudication. |
| Forensic laboratory error controversies | Illustrative | Expertise needs chain-of-custody, validation, and adversarial checking before it can responsibly settle a dispute. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** If the science is sound, courts should simply follow it, because legal filtering only distorts technical truth. **Response:** Courts decide particular rights under deadlines, burdens, and fairness constraints that science neither owns nor can discharge by itself.
