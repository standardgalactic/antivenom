## Claim
Freedom includes protected indeterminacy: the right not to have one's social meaning exhaustively fixed by a partial record.

## Depends on
- ch097–ch104 (Part XVII, Designing for Delayed Truth) — institutions should preserve revisability, layered judgment, bounded transparency, attention-aware memory, regular procedure, rights constraints, and asymmetric audit so they can lawfully say "not yet" before irreversible action.
- ch033 (The Right to Remain Underdescribed) — free persons need protection against enforced exhaustiveness in dossiers, profiles, and standardized self-explanations.
- ch046 (Delay, Liberty, and the Protected Interval) — liberty often depends on preserving time between suspicion and irreversible action.
- ch076 (The Freedom Region) — freedom is a zone carved out by limits on observation, inference, and brittle institutional updating rather than by noninterference alone.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Because social records are always partial, freedom includes the ability not to have one's social meaning exhaustively fixed by whatever record happens to be available first.
2. Because the chapter names this protection “protected indeterminacy,” liberty here depends on institutions refusing to convert incomplete descriptions into total identities.
3. Because this indeterminacy does not erase responsibility, the point is not to abolish judgment but to stop partial dossiers from becoming final personhood.
4. Therefore a free order preserves room for revision, surprise, and underdescription within institutional life.

| Case | Role | Note |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** Indeterminacy is often just vulnerability in polite language; if institutions refuse to classify decisively, the weak may bear the cost in insecurity and arbitrary treatment.
- **Response:** The chapter's claim is about protected indeterminacy, where law structures openness with rights and review rather than abandoning people to unmanaged uncertainty.
