# Spine: ch003 — The First Available Story

**Volume:** I: The Epistemology of Suspicion
**Part:** I. The Power of Paranoia
**Status:** spine

## Claim
The first available story gains disproportionate authority through the reinforcement dynamics of \(w_i(t+1)=(1-\lambda)w_i(t)+\lambda s_i(t)+\rho a_i(t)\), producing \emph{narrative hysteresis} even after the evidence shifts.

## Depends on
- ch001 — suspicion is warranted when more than one explanation remains live.
- ch002 — appearances underdetermine causes, so early closure is an epistemic temptation rather than an entitlement.

## Load-bearing steps
1. The chapter begins from the sequential arrival of hypotheses \(H_1,H_2,\ldots,H_n\), which gives temporal priority a formal place in later evidential competition.
2. Institutional uptake is modeled by the weight update \(w_i(t+1)=(1-\lambda)w_i(t)+\lambda s_i(t)+\rho a_i(t)\), where support \(s_i(t)\) and accumulated attention \(a_i(t)\) jointly determine a story's standing.
3. When \(\rho\) is large, attention becomes partially self-validating, explaining how indexing, repetition, search ranking, and resource allocation can amplify an early account apart from its truth.
4. That amplification is named \textbf{narrative hysteresis}: even after the evidence shifts, institutions can remain trapped in the original interpretive basin.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- The first story often wins simply because it is formed closest to the event and therefore incorporates the freshest evidence, not because institutions unfairly privilege it.
- Response: temporal proximity can help, but the chapter's point is that early uptake alters the evidential field itself, so later disagreement must overcome anchoring effects in addition to supplying better evidence.
