# Spine: ch021 — The Half-Life of an Initial Narrative

**Volume:** II: Causal Explosion
**Part:** IV. Time, Delay, and the Maturation of Evidence
**Status:** spine

## Claim
The half-life of an initial narrative is captured by \(N(t)=N_0e^{-\delta t}+\int_0^t \rho(s)e^{-\delta(t-s)}\,ds\), showing how repetition and institutional reinforcement preserve first stories beyond their evidentiary warrant.

## Depends on
- ch017 — warrant changes with evidentiary maturity over time.
- ch018 — long accumulations of evidence are often stronger than vivid episodes.
- ch019 — salience hierarchies give early stories disproportionate uptake.
- ch020 — discovery curves explain why later evidence can rationally outrun first impressions.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Because narrative persistence follows \(N(t)=N_0e^{-\delta t}+\int_0^t \rho(s)e^{-\delta(t-s)}\,ds\), an initial story decays at rate \(\delta\) only while ongoing reinforcement \(\rho(s)\) is not replenishing it.
2. Because institutions, media repetition, paperwork, and reputational commitments make \(\rho(s)\) positive, first narratives retain authority far longer than a simple forgetting model would predict.
3. Because later evidence must enter an attention environment already organized by a high \(N(t)\), correction is asymmetrically harder than initial accusation or framing.
4. Because the problem is structural rather than merely psychological, healthy procedure must damp reinforcement, preserve defeasibility, and create channels for revision before the initial narrative becomes effectively immortal.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** Without a stable initial narrative, institutions cannot coordinate action at all, so shortening narrative half-life invites chaos. **Response:** The chapter preserves provisional working stories but insists they be procedurally tagged as defeasible rather than prematurely canonical.
