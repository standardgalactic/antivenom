# Spine: ch015 — Computational Irreducibility and Legal Fact

**Volume:** II: Causal Explosion
**Part:** III. The Combinatorics of Context
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Where social processes are computationally irreducible, legally relevant fact cannot be compressed into an ex ante shortcut and must be learned through unfolding interaction under intractability, opacity, and state incompleteness.

- ch014 — the quality of a judgment rule depends on whether it fits the environment's actual complexity.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Because some processes reveal legally relevant consequences only by unfolding, no ex ante summary can replace running the system without losing the very structure the law cares about.
2. Because the chapter distinguishes hard undecidability from practical intractability, it can explain why NP-hard search, model uncertainty, chaotic sensitivity, strategic opacity, and incomplete state specification all matter without exaggerating them into magic.
3. Because those limits make prediction and front-loaded fact-finding systematically lossy, apparent rigor can simply be premature compression of an irreducible process.
4. Because irreducible environments still require action, the proper response is staged observation, revisable findings, and procedures that acknowledge what could not honestly have been known faster than the process itself unfolded.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** Courts and agencies must decide on the record they have, so talk of irreducibility is an evasion of the need for determinate findings. **Response:** The chapter concedes the need to decide but argues that irreducibility limits what kind of certainty is honest and therefore supports provisional, revisable procedures.
