# Spine: ch108 — Publicity of Power, Privacy of Persons

**Volume:** X: Normative Conclusions
**Part:** XVIII. Freedom Under Causal Opacity
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Coercive institutions should become more inspectable as their power grows, while persons should not become more exposed merely because institutions can observe more.

## Depends on
- ch107 (Equality of Evidentiary Standing) — people cannot contest power as equals if they are made transparent while governors remain analytically veiled.
- ch029 (Privacy Beyond Secrecy) — privacy is a boundary within which agency forms without constant extraction.
- ch075 (Privacy–Publicity Duality) — privacy and publicity are paired conditions that co-produce reliable evidence and legitimate scrutiny.
- ch103 (Audit Without Total Surveillance) — accountability requires auditability of power rather than blanket visibility of the governed.
- ch092 (When Privacy Conceals Domination) — the upward asymmetry must be defended without romanticizing every opaque social space as liberty-preserving.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Because institutions exercising coercive power claim authority over others, their rules, reasons, and interventions should become increasingly inspectable as their power grows.
2. Because persons do not ordinarily owe the public exhaustive legibility, greater institutional capacity to observe should not automatically produce greater exposure of private life.
3. Because the chapter's core asymmetry is upward publicity and downward privacy, accountability is aimed at power while personhood retains shelter from total observation.
4. Therefore the normative orientation of the book is a polity in which inspectability rises with coercive capacity, but personal exposure does not.

## External cases / technical analogues

| Case | Role | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Open-records or freedom-of-information obligations for official decision processes | Illustrative | Exemplifies publicity aimed at state action rather than at the intimate life of subjects. |
| Secret ballot protections | Illustrative | Shows a democratic institution that depends on privacy for persons even while electoral administration remains publicly scrutinizable. |

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** Private actors and networks can wield enormous power too, so drawing the publicity line mainly around formal institutions misses where domination often occurs.
- **Response:** The chapter can concede that power outside the state sometimes warrants exposure while maintaining the baseline asymmetry that ordinary persons are not presumptive objects of inspection.
