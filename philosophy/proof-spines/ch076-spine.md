# Spine: ch076 — The Freedom Region

**Volume:** VII: Formal Synthesis
**Part:** XIII. A Mathematics of Disciplined Distrust
**Status:** spine

## Claim
The freedom region is the feasible set $\mathfrak F=\{(\kappa,\rho,\phi,\theta):L_{\mathrm{domination}}+L_{\mathrm{exposure}}+L_{\mathrm{error}}+L_{\mathrm{inaction}}\leq\varepsilon\}$, treating liberty as a jointly maintained region rather than a scalar score.

## Depends on
- ch073 (Evidentiary Reach) — limits how far reputational and interpretive inferences may travel from any given datum
- ch074 (Update Budgets) — constrains how much institutions may revise a person's standing on the basis of new evidence
- ch075 (Privacy–Publicity Duality) — supplies the paired informational conditions that preserve both protected opacity and public answerability

## Load-bearing steps
1. Define the feasible set $\mathfrak F$ over the parameters $(\kappa,\rho,\phi,\theta)$ rather than over a single liberty score.
2. Use the inequality $L_{\mathrm{domination}}+L_{\mathrm{exposure}}+L_{\mathrm{error}}+L_{\mathrm{inaction}}\leq\varepsilon$ to aggregate the distinct failure modes that bound legitimate institutional life.
3. Interpret membership in $\mathfrak F$ as the joint maintenance of constraints against domination, overexposure, mistaken intervention, and paralyzing nonresponse.
4. Emphasize that different societies may occupy different points in $\mathfrak F$ without reducing all values to one commensurable optimum.
5. Conclude that freedom is the protected region left open when privacy-publicity constraints, reach limits, and update discipline are maintained together.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** Freedom cannot be captured by a region or threshold model; reducing liberty to managed uncertainty ignores agency, dignity, and material conditions.
- **Response:** The model is partial rather than exhaustive: it isolates the structural conditions under which people are not epistemically dominated, without claiming to define the whole value of freedom.
