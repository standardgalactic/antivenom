# Spine: ch073 — Evidentiary Reach

**Volume:** VII: Formal Synthesis
**Part:** XIII. A Mathematics of Disciplined Distrust
**Status:** spine

## Claim
Evidentiary reach is the set $\mathcal R(E)$ of propositions whose likelihoods are materially changed by $E$ across robust model variation, separating warranted inferential travel from mere vividness.

## Depends on
- ch071 (The Paranoia Functional) — frames responsible belief as preserving plurality unless evidence can justify broader commitment
- ch072 (Premature-Collapse Loss) — shows why overextending evidence into domains it cannot bear generates systematic harm rather than a mere technical mistake

## Load-bearing steps
1. Define the reach set by $\mathcal R(E)=\{H:\inf_{M\in\mathfrak M}|P_M(H\mid E)-P_M(H)|>\epsilon\}$, so only propositions whose likelihood shifts robustly across admissible models count as genuinely reached by $E$.
2. Use the model class $\mathfrak M$ and threshold $\epsilon$ to distinguish tested inferential movement from claims that change only under fragile modeling assumptions.
3. Interpret membership in $\mathcal R(E)$ as a bounded warrant: evidence may strongly affect some propositions while leaving neighboring narratives, character judgments, or policy generalizations outside its legitimate scope.
4. Contrast reach with vividness by showing that emotional or rhetorical salience does not substitute for robust model-sensitive likelihood change.
5. Use $\mathcal R(E)$ to ground the principle that later update magnitudes must be proportional to tested reach rather than to the mere force of presentation.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** Existing notions like relevance and probative value already govern how evidence is used, so "reach" may just rename familiar evidentiary doctrines without adding anything.
- **Response:** Relevance is often local to a proposition; reach names the separate problem of inferential spillover across contexts, persons, and decision layers.
