# Spine: ch018 — Millions of Hours

**Volume:** II: Causal Explosion
**Part:** IV. Time, Delay, and the Maturation of Evidence
**Status:** spine

## Claim
The epistemic force of millions of hours is structured by serial review \(V/r\) and backlog dynamics under arrival rate \(\lambda\), review rate \(\mu\), and escalation fraction \(p\), so duration can be probative even when institutions struggle to process it. This is the chapter's worked case of \textbf{capacity delay} from ch017's taxonomy: no evidence is withheld and nothing is gained by waiting, throughput is simply insufficient.

## Depends on
- ch017 — responsible belief depends on when in an inquiry one stands because evidence matures over time.

## Load-bearing steps
1. Because a record of size \(V\) reviewed at effective rate \(r\) demands serial attention \(V/r\), sheer volume can make abundant evidence functionally inaccessible.
2. Because parallelization introduces coordination, duplication, labeling, and escalation costs, raw recording capacity does not automatically translate into usable judgment.
3. Because evidence items arrive at rate \(\lambda\), are processed at rate \(\mu\), and only a fraction \(p\) are escalated, the relevant bottleneck is a queueing system rather than a simple archive.
4. Because backlog grows whenever effective arrival pressure exceeds review capacity, institutions need designs for harvesting long-duration evidence instead of assuming that comprehensive surveillance equals comprehensive understanding.

## Objections this chapter must survive
- **Objection:** Aggregated hours merely multiply the same blind spots, so duration can entrench systematic error instead of correcting it. **Response:** The chapter grants this and therefore treats audited collection and understood denominators as conditions for duration's evidentiary force.
