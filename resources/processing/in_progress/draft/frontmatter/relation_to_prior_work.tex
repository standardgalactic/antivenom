\chapter*{Relation to Prior Work}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Relation to Prior Work}

This monograph sits downstream of four prior works, from which it
imports definitions and one theorem without modification, and against
which it states its own extensions precisely.

From \emph{Observability Is Restorability} this monograph imports the
basic repair-theoretic vocabulary: repair operators, the repair-stable
domain $\restdomain$, and the repair interference functional
$\interf{d_i}{d_j}$. From \emph{Admissibility Before Truth} it imports
the principle that truth (and, in this monograph, sufficiency) is
defined only over repair-stable distinctions. From \emph{The Geometry
of Reconstruction} it imports the observation that repair operators
themselves require higher-order maintenance, motivating this
monograph's treatment of confidence profiles as themselves renderings
subject to loss (\cref{prop:confidence-renderings}).

From \emph{Conservation of Ambiguity} this monograph imports the
Redistribution Theorem in full and without modification
(\cref{thm:redistribution}), together with its explicit corollary that
ambiguity conservation is only a degenerate limiting case of the more
general repair-budget conservation. This monograph does \emph{not}
claim to be a corollary of that theorem. The Redistribution Theorem
governs a single observer's competing distinctions at one moment; this
monograph's Composition Failure Theorem
(\cref{thm:composition-failure}) governs a single distinction's status
as it moves through a sequence of independently-budgeted stages over
time. Appendix~\ref{app:comparison} gives the side-by-side comparison
in full, including a worked demonstration that one result is not
derivable from the other without the additional budget-relay
machinery developed in Part~II.

Distinguishability Geometry and Admissibility Theory are imported more
informally, as the native vocabulary for, respectively, what survives
a projection (\cref{ch:distinctions}) and what continuations remain
reachable given a substrate (\cref{ch:admissibility}). Neither prior
work is modified here; both are specialized to the continuation
problem this monograph addresses.
