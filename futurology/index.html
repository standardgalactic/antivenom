Alicia Juarrero is an American philosopher whose research addresses complex systems, the philosophy of action, causation, and neuroethics. Her work applies dynamical systems and constraint-based explanations to questions about intentional behavior, agency, and the emergence of higher-level organization.

Juarrero wrote Dynamics in Action: Intentional Behavior as a Complex System (1999) and Context Changes Everything: How Constraints Create Coherence (2023), both published by MIT Press. Her work has received critical discussion in philosophy journals, including The Philosophical Review, The British Journal for the Philosophy of Science, and Philosophical Psychology.[3][4][5]

Education and academic career

Juarrero completed doctoral studies in philosophy at the University of Miami.[1] She taught philosophy at Prince George's Community College in Maryland and subsequently held emerita status.

She has been affiliated with the Neuroethics Studies Program of the Pellegrino Center for Clinical Bioethics at Georgetown University Medical Center, where her research interests have included models of neural processes related to moral cognition, emotion, and behavior.[2]

Biographical accounts also report that Juarrero served on the National Council on the Humanities from 1992 to 2000 and chaired a committee concerning state humanities programs.[1]

Philosophical work

Complex systems and intentional action

In Dynamics in Action, Juarrero examines whether conventional linear models of causation can adequately explain intentional action. She draws on feedback, nonlinear dynamics, self-organization, and the behavior of complex systems to develop an account of actions as temporally extended and organized processes.[6]

The approach treats intentions and actions in relation to the dynamic organization of agents rather than as isolated events connected only by a sequence of mechanical causes. It therefore connects the philosophy of action with questions of emergence and the organization of physical and cognitive processes.

Causality as constraint

In her 1998 paper “Causality as Constraint,” Juarrero argues that constraints do more than restrict a system's available states. Context-sensitive constraints can also enable new forms of organization, allowing a higher-level system to exhibit capacities unavailable to its isolated components.[7]

She distinguishes context-independent constraints from context-sensitive constraints, using examples from information theory, language, autocatalytic chemistry, and dissipative systems. The latter alter conditional relationships among components and can thereby contribute to the emergence of new systemic properties.[7]

Emergence and downward causation

Juarrero's analysis distinguishes the formation of an organized whole from the subsequent effects of that whole's organization on its constituent parts. On this account, relations among components enable higher-level organization, while the established system constrains the possible behavior of those components. Her discussion connects this reciprocal organization to longstanding debates about emergence, part–whole relations, and downward causation.[7]

Context and coherence

Her 2023 book Context Changes Everything develops a broader account of how constraints create and sustain coherence across complex systems. It examines enabling, constitutive, and governing constraints, as well as questions of identity, persistence, and context-dependent organization.[8]

Scholarly reception

Dynamics in Action received reviews in several academic philosophy journals. Muhammad Ali Khalidi reviewed it in The Philosophical Review in 2001; Frederick Adams reviewed it in The British Journal for the Philosophy of Science; and Brandon N. Towl published a critical review in Philosophical Psychology.[3][4][5] These reviews document engagement with her approach to intentional action and the use of complex systems theory in philosophical explanation.

Her work has also been discussed in connection with Michael Polanyi's philosophy and in interdisciplinary conversations about complex adaptive systems.[9] Public interviews have addressed her later theory of constraints and its implications for cognition and social organization.[10][11]

Other activities

Juarrero has been associated with VectorAnalytica, a consultancy concerned with complex systems analysis. She has also participated in public discussions of complexity science, philosophy, and organizational practice.[12]
